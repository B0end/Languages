# The Nominative Case

The **Nominative Case** is your ground zero: it is the **default dictionary form** of every noun, adjective, and pronoun in Russian. 

It never changes for prepositions and never serves as a direct object. It is the pure, unaltered anchor of the sentence:

```text
                           ┌── Singular Nouns (Base gender: Masc, Fem, Neut)
                           ├── Plural Nouns (-ы, -и, -а, -я)
                           ├── Modifiers (Demonstratives & Adjectives)
NOMINATIVE CASE ───────────┼── Subject Pronouns (Я, Ты, Он, Она...)
(Who / What acts)          ├── Present "Zero-Copula" (Subject = Predicate)
                           └── Possession with "У меня есть..." (The item is the subject!)
```

It answers only two fundamental questions:
* [Кто?;; Who? / ¿Quién?](Кто.wav) — For **animate** entities *(people, animals, living beings)*.
* [Что?;; What? / ¿Qué?](Что.wav) — For **inanimate** entities *(objects, places, abstract concepts)*.

---

## When Do You Use the Nominative?

Use the Nominative case whenever a word fits one of these **four core roles**:

### 1. The Active Agent (The Grammatical Subject)
The person or thing performing the action of the verb:
* [Студент учится;; The student studies / El estudiante estudia](StudentUchitsya.wav)
* [Поезд прибывает;; The train is arriving / El tren llega](ПоездПрибывает.wav)

---

### 2. The Present-Tense Equation ($A = B$)
In modern Russian, the verb "to be" (*am, is, are*) is omitted in the present tense (the **Zero Copula**). Both sides of the equation stay in the Nominative:

Subject [Nominative] = Identity [Nominative]

* [Он врач;; He is a doctor / Él es médico](OnVrach.wav) *(Lit: "He — doctor")*
* [Москва — красивый город;; Moscow is a beautiful city / Moscú es una ciudad hermosa](МоскваКрасивыйГород.wav)

---

### 3. Possession with *«У [someone] есть...»*
In Russian, you do not say *"I have a car"*. Instead, you say:  
> **"At me, there exists a car."**

Because the item you own is the thing that *exists*, **the possessed object is the grammatical subject** and must be in the Nominative case:

* [У меня есть машина;; I have a car / Tengo un carro](UMenyaEstMashina.wav) *(Машина = Nominative Subject)*
* [У него есть брат;; He has a brother / Él tiene un hermano](УНегоЕстьБрат.wav) *(Брат = Nominative Subject)*

---

### 4. Standalone Labels, Signs & Titles
Anything existing in isolation without a verb—such as street signs, store names, book titles, and directory labels—is automatically in the Nominative:
* [Аптека;; Pharmacy / Farmacia](Аптека.wav)
* [Метро;; Metro, Subway / Metro](Метро.wav)
* [Библиотека;; Library / Biblioteca](Библиотека.wav)
