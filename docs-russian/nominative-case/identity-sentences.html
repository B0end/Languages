Identity Sentences

Identity sentences (also called equational or copular sentences) are sentences where you state that "A is B" (e.g., “I am a student”, “Madrid is the capital”, “He is my brother”).

In Spanish, you rely on the verb ser (Soy médico, Esto es un error). In English, you use the verb to be (I am a doctor, This is a mistake).

In Russian, this structure comes with a famous surprise: there is NO verb "to be" in the present tense.

1. Present Tense Identity (The Zero Copula)​

In the present tense, the Russian verb быть;; ser, estar / to be (to be / ser) disappears completely. You simply place the Subject next to the Predicate.

Both parts are in the Nominative Case (dictionary form): Subject (Nominative) + Predicate (Nominative)

Good news for Spanish speakers:​

Just like in Spanish (Soy estudiante vs. English I am a student), Russian has no indefinite articles (un, una / a, an). You don't need to add anything extra!

Examples with Pronouns:​

  • Я студент;; Soy estudiante / I am a student
  • Она инженер;; Ella es ingeniera / She is an engineer
  • Он мой лучший друг;; Él es mi mejor amigo / He is my best friend
  • Мы испанцы;; Somos españoles / We are Spanish

2. The Em-Dash rule: "Noun — Noun"​

When both the subject and the predicate are nouns (rather than personal pronouns like Я or Он), Russian spelling rules require a long dash (—).

Think of this dash as visually taking the place of the Spanish word es or English is:

  • Анна — врач;; Anna es médica / Anna is a doctor
  • Москва — столица России;; Moscú es la capital de Rusia / Moscow is the capital of Russia
  • Мой папа — архитектор;; Mi papá es arquitecto / My dad is an architect
note

When the subject is a pronoun like Я, Ты, Он, you do not need the dash.

3. Definitions with "— это" (Esto es...)​

When defining concepts or making broad identity statements, Russians often combine the dash with the demonstrative это;; esto / this. This translates directly to Spanish "es..." or "significa..." and English "is...":

  • Время — это деньги;; El tiempo es dinero / Time is money
  • Собака — это друг человека;; El perro es el amigo del hombre / A dog is man's best friend
  • Мадрид — это большой город;; Madrid es una ciudad grande / Madrid is a big city

4. Negative Identity Sentences​

To say someone or something is not something, simply place the negative particle не;; no / not directly before the predicate noun.

  • Я не студент;; No soy estudiante / I am not a student
  • Он не врач;; Él no es médico / He is not a doctor
  • Это не моя ошибка;; Este no es mi error / This is not my mistake
  • Москва не страна;; Moscú no es un país / Moscow is not a country

5. Past and Future Identity​

While the verb быть;; ser, estar / to be (ser/estar) does not exist in the present tense, it reappears in the past and future:

  • Past: был;; fue, era / was (masc), была;; fue, era / was (fem), было;; fue, era / was (neut), были;; fueron, eran / were (plural).
  • Future: буду;; seré / I will be, будешь;; serás / you will be, будет;; será / he, she, it will be, будем;; seremos / we will be, будете;; serán / you will be, будут;; serán / they will be.

Here comes the Russian twist:

When using быть in the past or future to describe a profession, temporary identity, or role, the predicate noun changes from the Nominative case into the Instrumental Case.

Think of the Instrumental case here as equivalent to the Spanish idea of "trabajar de [médico]" or "actuar como [estudiante]":

Past Tense:​

  • В прошлом году я был студентом;; El año pasado fui estudiante / Last year I was a student (студент;; estudiante / student → студентом;; estudiante / student [Instrumental])
  • Она была хорошей учительницей;; Ella era una buena profesora / She was a good teacher (учительница;; profesora / teacher → учительницей;; profesora / teacher [Instrumental])
  • Они были друзьями;; Ellos eran amigos / They were friends (друзья;; amigos / friends → друзьями;; amigos / friends [Instrumental])

Future Tense:​

  • Я буду врачом;; Seré médico / I will be a doctor (врач;; médico / doctor → врачом;; médico / doctor [Instrumental])
  • Мой сын будет инженером;; Mi hijo será ingeniero / My son will be an engineer (инженер;; ingeniero / engineer → инженером;; ingeniero / engineer [Instrumental])
