Cyrillic Alphabet (33 Letters)

We can break the alphabet into four distinct categories:

Group 1: The "True Friends"​

These look and sound roughly the same as in Spanish/English.

LetterRepresentative ExampleSpanish / English equivalent
А аАптека;; Farmacia / PharmacyLike the Spanish A (casa)
Е еЕда;; Comida / FoodLike Spanish "ye" in ayer (or English yes)
К кКот;; Gato / CatLike C or K (kilo)
М мМама;; Mamá / MomLike M (madre)
О оОстров;; Isla / IslandLike the pure Spanish O (oso)
Т тТорт;; Pastel, tarta / CakeLike T (tomate)

Group 2: The "False Friends"​

These look like Latin letters, but represent COMPLETELY different sounds.

LetterRepresentative ExampleExplanation & Equivalents
В вВино;; Vino / WineSounds like the English "V" (vine), not the Spanish B/V. Bite your lower lip.
Н нНос;; Nariz / NoseIt looks like an H, but it is an N (noche).
Р рРыба;; Pez, pescado / FishIt looks like a P, but it is an R. A rolled R like Spanish pero or perro.
С сСок;; Zumo, jugo / JuiceIt looks like a C, but it is ALWAYS an S (sol).
У уУтро;; Mañana / MorningIt looks like a Y, but it is a U (Spanish uno, English boot).
Х хХлеб;; Pan / BreadIt looks like an X, but it is the Spanish "J" (jamón, México).

Group 3: The "Greek Strangers"​

LetterRepresentative ExampleOrigin & Equivalents
Г гГитара;; Guitarra / GuitarSounds like the hard G in Spanish gato.
Д дДом;; Casa / HouseSounds like D in Spanish dado.
Л лЛуна;; Luna / MoonSounds like L in Spanish luna.
П пПарк;; Parque / ParkSounds like P in Spanish papá.
Ф фФото;; Foto / PhotoSounds like F in Spanish fuego.

Group 4: The "Slavic Specials"​

These were invented specifically to represent Slavic sounds that Greek couldn't express.

LetterRepresentative ExampleBest Explanation for Spanish/English Speakers
Б бБабушка;; Abuela / GrandmotherThe actual B sound (Spanish barco).
Ж жЖурнал;; Revista / MagazineLike English "measure" or French "je". If you speak Argentine Spanish, it's identical to the Argentine pronunciation of "ll" or "y" (calle, yo).
З зЗоопарк;; Zoológico / ZooIt's the voiced English "Z" (zebra), vibrating like a bee.
И иИгра;; Juego / GameSounds like Spanish I (isla).
Й йЧай;; Té / TeaCalled "Short I". It acts like the Spanish Y in diphthongs (hoy, rey).
Ц цЦентр;; Centro / CenterLike the "zz" in pizza, or "ts" in English cats.
Ч чЧас;; Hora / HourSounds exactly like the Spanish "CH" (chico).
Ш шШоколад;; Chocolate / ChocolateLike the English "SH" (show, shadow).
Щ щБорщ;; Borscht (sopa) / BorschtA softer, longer "shch". Like saying Spanish pesh-chico very fast.
Ы ыСыр;; Queso / CheeseA guttural "i". Imagine being punched in the stomach while trying to say the Spanish "u", but forcing your mouth into an "i" shape.
Э эЭто;; Esto / ThisA "hard" E. Sounds like the pure Spanish E (elefante).
Ю юЮг;; Sur / SouthSounds like "yu" (like the English word you).
Я яЯблоко;; Manzana / AppleSounds like "ya" (Spanish ya, yate).

The Silent Modifiers: Ъ and Ь​

Cyrillic has two letters that have no sound of their own. They are instructions to the speaker on how to pronounce the consonant that comes right before them:

  1. Soft Sign (Мягкий знак - Ь ь):

    • Tells you to palatalize the previous consonant.
    • Examples:
      • День;; Día / Day (Softens the Н into a sound like Spanish ñ)
      • Соль;; Sal / Salt (Softens the Л into a sound like Castilian Spanish ll)

  2. Hard Sign (Твёрдый знак - Ъ ъ):

    • Tells you to NOT soften the consonant, creating a sharp separation or pause between a consonant and the following vowel.
    • Example:
      • Объект;; Objeto / Object (Prevents the б from blending into the е, sounding like "ob-yekt")
