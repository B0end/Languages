The 3 Tenses & 2 Aspects

In English and Spanish, you juggle dozens of compound and continuous forms (había leído, habré estado leyendo, would have read, had been reading).

In Russian, grammar is radically streamlined: there are only 3 tenses (Past, Present, Future) and 2 Aspects (Imperfective vs. Perfective). Together, they produce exactly 5 functional verb forms:

The 5-Form Matrix​

TenseImperfective (НСВ)
Process • Habit • Duration		Perfective (СВ)
Result • Limit • Completion
PastЯ читал;; Yo leía / I was reading
(Ongoing activity in the past)		Я прочитал;; Yo leí / I finished reading
(Completed event with a result)
PresentЯ читаю;; Yo leo, estoy leyendo / I read, I am reading
(Happening right now or regularly)		❌ Does Not Exist (See explanation below)
FutureЯ буду читать;; Yo estaré leyendo / I will be reading
(Compound: быть + infinitive)		Я прочитаю;; Yo leeré / I will read completely
(Simple: conjugated like a present tense)
tip

Why is there no "Present Perfective"?

The Present is an open window: an action happening right now is inherently in progress (uncompleted).

If an action reaches completion, it is either already in the Past ("I finished it") or projected into the Future ("I will finish it").

Because of this, when you attach present-tense endings to a Perfective stem, it automatically refers to the Future!

Core Concept: Aspect Mindset​

Russian verb aspect answers a fundamental question: Are we looking at the journey or the destination?

Imperfective Aspect (Несовершенный вид – НСВ)​

  • Focus: The process, duration, repetition, or general activity.
  • Core Question: Что делал? ("What were you doing?")
  • Native Equivalents:
    • Spanish: Pretérito Imperfecto (leía, hacía) or continuous (estaba leyendo).
    • English: Past Continuous (was reading) or simple habitual (used to read).

Perfective Aspect (Совершенный вид – СВ)​

  • Focus: Concrete results, total completion, single-instance actions, or reaching a boundary/finish line.
  • Core Question: Что сделал? ("What did you accomplish / finish?")
  • Native Equivalents:
    • Spanish: Pretérito Indefinido (leí, hice).
    • English: Past Simple (with completed nuance) or Present Perfect (read to completion, have finished).

Aspect Twin Formation​

Russian verbs exist in matched pairs: [Imperfective Twin] ⇄ [Perfective Twin]. These pairs are formed using three main mechanisms:

                  ┌── 1. PREFIXATION:         Делать (IPF)    ──(+ с-)──>   Сделать (PF)
                  │
   VERB PAIR  ────┼── 2. SUFFIXATION / SHIFT: Открыть (PF)   ──(+ -ва-)─>  Открывать (IPF)
                  │                           Решить (PF)    ──(и ➔ а)──>  Решать (IPF)
                  │
                  └── 3. SUPPLETION:          Говорить (IPF) ──(root)───>  Сказать (PF)

Pattern 1: Pure Prefixation (IPF ➔ PF)​

Adding an "empty" prefix to an imperfective base to mark completion without changing the basic meaning of the verb:

  • Делать;; Hacer / To do ➡️ с- ➡️ Сделать;; Hacer y terminar / To complete
  • Писать;; Escribir / To write ➡️ на- ➡️ Написать;; Terminar de escribir / To write down completely
  • Пить;; Beber / To drink ➡️ вы- ➡️ Выпить;; Beberse todo / To drink up
  • Читать;; Leer / To read ➡️ про- ➡️ Прочитать;; Leer de principio a fin / To read through

Pattern 2: Suffix Mutation & Shifts​

Often, adding suffixes like -ыва-/-ива- or -ва- derives an imperfective from a perfective verb (secondary imperfectivization), or a vowel shift distinguishes the pair:

  • Открыть;; Abrir (resultado) / To open ➡️ -ва- ➡️ Открывать;; Abrir (proceso/hábito) / To be opening
  • Купить;; Comprar (hecho puntual) / To buy ➡️ Покупать;; Comprar (proceso/hábito) / To buy
  • Решить;; Resolver, decidir / To solve, decide ➡️ -а- ➡️ Решать;; Intentar resolver / To be solving

Pattern 3: Suppletion (Completely Different Roots)​

Just like Spanish ir ➔ fui or English go ➔ went, high-frequency verbs use entirely different roots for each aspect:

  • Говорить;; Hablar (proceso) / To speak ⇄ Сказать;; Decir (puntual) / To say, tell
  • Брать;; Tomar, coger (proceso) / To take ⇄ Взять;; Tomar, agarrar (hecho) / To seize, take

   IMPERFECTIVE TRIGGERS                         PERFECTIVE TRIGGERS
   (Repeated, Habitual, Ongoing)                 (Single, Decisive, Result-oriented)
   ──────────────────────────────────            ──────────────────────────────────
   Всегда        (Siempre)                       Вдруг         (De repente)
   Каждый день   (Todos los días)                Наконец       (Por fin / Al fin)
   Часто         (A menudo)                      Сразу         (De inmediato)
   Долго         (Por mucho tiempo)              Уже           (Ya - completed)

Negation Logic: Aspect Shifts the Meaning​

Placing не (not) before each aspect radically alters your message:

  1. Не + Imperfective = General Lack of Desire / Total Prohibition / Don't Bother:

    • Не делай этого;; No lo hagas (bajo ningún concepto)
    • Я не пил кофе;; No tomé café (no es parte de mi rutina / no quise)

  2. Не + Perfective = Failed Attempt (Tried, but couldn't get the result):

    • Я не сделал домашку;; No logré terminar la tarea (lo intenté o debí, pero fallé)
    • Не забудь!;; ¡No te olvides! (evita el fallo de olvidar)

Quick Decision Framework​

When choosing a verb form in Russian, ask yourself:

  1. Is the action happening right now?
    • Yes ➔ Present Imperfective (Я читаю).
  2. Did it happen in the past?
    • Was it an ongoing background action or repeated habit? ➔ Past Imperfective (Я читал).
    • Did you cross the finish line and reach a result? ➔ Past Perfective (Я прочитал).
  3. Will it happen in the future?
    • Will you be in the middle of doing it, or doing it repeatedly? ➔ Future Imperfective (Я буду читать).
    • Will you get it done and achieve a result? ➔ Future Perfective (Я прочитаю).
