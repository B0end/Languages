Past Tense

The Past Tense does NOT conjugate by person (Я, Ты, Он). Instead, the past tense behaves exactly like an adjective: it conjugates purely by GENDER (masculine, feminine, neuter) and NUMBER (singular vs. plural).

The Formula​

  1. Take the infinitive: Читать;; Leer
  2. Drop -ть ➡️ Чита-
  3. Glue on the gender marker:
Subject CategoryEndingFormula with ЧитатьResult
Masculine (Él, o un hombre hablando)-лЧита- + -лОн читал;; Él leía / leyó
Feminine (Ella, o una mujer hablando)-лаЧита- + -лаОна читала;; Ella leía / leyó
Neuter (Sustantivos neutros: окно, дело)-лоЧита- + -лоОно читало;; Leía / leyó
Plural (Мы, Вы, Они)-лиЧита- + -лиОни читали;; Ellos leían / leyeron

First and Second Person Gender Matching:​

  • Man speaking: Я знал;; Yo sabía / I knew
  • Woman speaking: Я знала;; Yo sabía / I knew
  • Asking a man: Ты был там?;; ¿Estuviste allí? / Were you there?
  • Asking a woman: Ты была там?;; ¿Estuviste allí? / Were you there?

The Return of "TO BE" (Быть)​

While absent in the present, быть is required in the past:

  • Он был дома;; Él estaba en casa
  • Она была дома;; Ella estaba en casa
  • Это было хорошо;; Estuvo bien
  • Мы были дома;; Estábamos en casa
tip

Negative Stress Shift

In negative feminine past of быть, the stress leaps to the final letter:
Она не была́;; Ella no estuvo / She was not (pronounced "ni-by-LÁ").

The Irregulars​

Most Russian verbs are 100% regular in the past tense. However, there are two common exceptions where the pattern shifts:

The Disappearing -Л in Masculine Consonant Stems​

For verbs whose stems end in a consonant (like Мочь;; Poder / To be able to), the masculine form drops the -л because it is too awkward to pronounce a consonant directly next to 'л':

GenderMasculine Drops -лResult
MasculineМог (NOT Могл)Он мог;; Él pudo / He could
FeminineМог + -лаОна могла;; Ella pudo / She could
NeuterМог + -лоОно могло;; Pudo / It could
PluralМог + -лиОни могли;; Ellos pudieron / They could

Other common verbs following this rule:

  • Умереть;; Morir / To die ➡️ Он умер;; Él murió / He died vs. Она умерла;; Ella murió / She died

The Completely Irregular: Идти (To Go on Foot)​

Just like Spanish ir ➡️ fui, the root of Идти;; Ir a pie / To walk completely changes to ш-:

  • Masculine: Он шёл домой;; Él iba a casa a pie / He was walking home
  • Feminine: Она шла домой;; Ella iba a casa a pie / She was walking home
  • Neuter: Время шло;; El tiempo pasaba / Time was passing
  • Plural: Они шли домой;; Ellos iban a casa a pie / They were walking home

Past Tense + Aspect​

Remember Verb Aspects? The past tense is where aspects shine most clearly for Spanish speakers:

  1. Imperfective Past (Pretérito Imperfecto - Habit or Process):
    • Я писал письмо;; Yo escribía una carta / I was writing a letter
    • Focuses on what you were doing, not if you finished.
  2. Perfective Past (Pretérito Indefinido - Completed Result):
    • Я написал письмо;; Escribí la carta (y la terminé) / I wrote the letter
    • Focuses on the finished milestone.

Possession in the past​

Replace есть with the past tense of быть:

  • Masculine object: У меня был кот;; Tuve/tenía un gato / I had a cat (masc)
  • Feminine object: У меня была машина;; Tuve/tenía un coche / I had a car (fem)
  • Neuter object: У меня было время;; Tuve tiempo / I had time (neut)
  • Plural objects: У меня были проблемы;; Tuve problemas / I had problems (plur)

Saying "I Didn't Have"​

Saying you lack something requires two things:

  1. The word НЕТ (No hay / There isn't).
  2. The item you don't have must change into the Genitive case!
  • У меня не было машины;; No tenía coche / I didn't have a car
  • У меня не было времени;; No tuve tiempo / I didn't have time
