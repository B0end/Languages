# The 6 Grammatical Cases

If you speak Spanish and English, you actually use cases every single day—you just don't realize it because you only apply them to **pronouns**, whereas Slavic languages apply them to **all nouns, adjectives, and pronouns**.

---

## What is a Case?

In English and Spanish, the role of a word in a sentence is determined by **word order** and **prepositions**:

* *The dog bites the man* ➡️ The dog is the attacker.
* *The man bites the dog* ➡️ The man is the attacker.
* *El perro muerde al hombre* ➡️ The preposition **"al"** tells you who is being bitten.

Now look at Spanish pronouns:
* You say: **Yo** tengo un libro (*Subject*)
* You don't say: *El perro me vio a **yo*** ➡️ You say: **Me** vio a **mí** (*Direct object*)
* You don't say: *Ven con **yo*** ➡️ You say: Ven **conmigo** (*Instrumental companion*)

**Yo ➡️ Me ➡️ Mí ➡️ Conmigo** are cases! Slavic languages simply took that exact same concept and said: *"Let's do that to EVERY noun by changing its ending."*

Because the ending reveals who is doing what, **word order in Russian is completely flexible**.

## Deep Dive into the 6 Cases

### 1. Nominative Case (Именительный) — *The Subject*
This is the default **dictionary form**. It is the person or thing performing the action.

* **Answers:** Кто? (Who?) / Что? (What?)
* **Spanish parallel:** El sujeto de la oración.
* [Студент читает книгу;; El estudiante lee un libro / The student reads a book](СтудентЧитаетКнигу.wav)
  (Студент is doing the reading, so it stays in the base Nominative form).

---

### 2. Accusative Case (Винительный) — *The Direct Object*
The person or thing directly receiving the action of the verb.

* **Answers:** Кого? (Whom?) / Что? (What?)
* **Spanish parallel:** El complemento directo (*¿Qué compro? ¿A quién veo?*).
* Look at feminine nouns ending in **-А**: they change to **-У**:
  * [Я вижу машину;; Veo un coche / I see a car](ЯВижуМашину.wav) (Машин**а** ➡️ Машин**у**)
  * [Я знаю Анну;; Conozco a Anna / I know Anna](ЯЗнаюАнну.wav) (Анн**а** ➡️ Анн**у**)

:::tip
Spanish, you say *"Veo el coche"* (inanimate), but *"Veo **a** María"* (animate person). Russian does the exact same thing! In the Accusative case, masculine living beings change their endings, while masculine non-living objects do not change at all.
:::

---

### 3. Genitive Case (Родительный) — *Possession & Absence*
This is the hardest-working case in the entire language. It does three major jobs:

1. **Possession (Spanish *de* / English *'s / of*):**
   * [Дом отца;; La casa del padre / Father's house](ДомОтца.wav)
2. **Absence (With the word "НЕТ" = *No hay / No está*):**
   * In Spanish: *No hay agua*. In Russian, whatever is missing MUST be in the Genitive case:
   * [Нет воды;; No hay agua / There is no water](НетВоды.wav) (Вод**а** ➡️ Вод**ы**)
   * [Ивана нет дома;; Iván no está en casa / Ivan is not home](ИванаНетДома.wav) (Иван ➡️ Иван**а**)
3. **Quantities & Numbers:** Used after quantities like *mucho, poco* or numbers 2, 3, 4...
   * [Много чая;; Mucho té / A lot of tea](МногоЧая.wav)

---

### 4. Dative Case (Дательный) — *The Indirect Object*
The root of this word comes from the verb **дать** (*dar / to give*). It is used for the recipient of an action.

* **Answers:** Кому? (To whom?) / Чему? (To what?)
* **Spanish parallel:** Complemento indirecto (*dar a, decir a, enviar a*).
* [Я даю книгу другу;; Le doy el libro a un amigo / I give the book to a friend](ЯДаюКнигуДругу.wav)
  (**Друг** [amigo] becomes **Друг-у** [al amigo]).
* [Я звоню маме;; Llamo a mamá / I call mom](ЯЗвонюМаме.wav)
  *(**Мама** becomes **Мам-е**).*

---

### 5. Instrumental Case (Творительный) — *With & By Means Of*
This case tells you the **tool** used to perform an action, or the **person accompanying you**.

* **Answers:** Кем? (With/by whom?) / Чем? (With/by what tool?)
* **Spanish parallel:** *Con qué instrumento* o *en compañía de quién* (*con*).
* **As an instrument (No preposition needed!):**
  * [Я пишу карандашом;; Escribo con un lápiz / I write with a pencil](ЯПишуКарандашом.wav)
    (**Карандаш** ➡️ **Карандаш-ом**. You don't even need a word for "with"! The ending literally means "by means of").
* **In company (With the preposition С = *con*):**
  * [Кофе с молоком;; Café con leche / Coffee with milk](КофеСМолоком.wav) (Молок**о** ➡️ Молок**ом**)
  * [Я говорю с Иваном;; Hablo con Iván / I speak with Ivan](ЯГоворюСИваном.wav) (Иван ➡️ Иван**ом**)

---

### 6. Prepositional Case (Предложный) — *Location & Topics*
This is the easiest case because **it NEVER appears alone**. It only exists when preceded by specific prepositions, mainly:
* **В / НА** = in, on, at *(Location)*
* **О / ОБ** = about *(Topic of speech/thought)*

* **Answers:** О ком? (About whom?) / О чём? (About what?) / Где? (Where?)
* **Ending:** Almost every singular noun simply takes the ending **-Е**:
  * [Я живу в Мадриде;; Vivo en Madrid / I live in Madrid](ЯЖивуВМадриде.wav) (Мадрид ➡️ Мадрид**е**)
  * [Мы думаем о России;; Pensamos en Rusia / We think about Russia](МыДумаемОРоссии.wav)
  * [Книга на столе;; El libro está en/sobre la mesa / The book is on the table](КнигаНаСтоле.wav) (Стол ➡️ Стол**е**)

## Why This Gives You Superpowers (Flexible Word Order)

Because the endings identify the role of each word, you can reorder a Russian sentence any way you want to emphasize different parts, without changing the basic meaning:

1. [Иван любит Анну;; Iván ama a Anna / Ivan loves Anna](ИванЛюбитАнну.wav) *(Neutral)*
2. [Анну любит Иван;; Es a Anna a quien Iván ama / It is Anna whom Ivan loves](АннуЛюбитИван.wav) *(Emphasis on Anna)*

Even though **Анну** is at the front of sentence #2, the **-у** ending proves 100% that she is still the object of affection, and **Иван** (with no ending) is still the one doing the loving. In Spanish or English, swapping the order would completely reverse who loves whom!

## Summary Checklist

1. **Nominative:** Who does it? ➡️ Base dictionary form.
2. **Accusative:** Who receives it? ➡️ Feminines end in **-у/-ю**, masculine animates change.
3. **Genitive:** Whose is it? Or what is missing? ➡️ Equivalent to Spanish **"de"** and **"no hay"**.
4. **Dative:** To whom do you give it? ➡️ Equivalent to Spanish **"a / para"**.
5. **Instrumental:** What do you do it with? ➡️ Equivalent to Spanish **"con"** (**-ом / -ой**).
6. **Prepositional:** Where is it? What are you talking about? ➡️ Prepositions **в, на, о** + ending **-е**.
