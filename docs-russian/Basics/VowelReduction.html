Vowel Reduction

When a vowel is NOT stressed, it loses its strength, changes its sound, and becomes shorter and weaker.

  • In Spanish, vowels are sacred. They never change. Whether a vowel is stressed or unstressed, an A is always /a/, and an O is always /o/. The words papa (potato) and papá (dad) use the exact same vowel sound; only the volume and pitch change. Spanish vowels are pure.
  • In English, vowels are "lazy." English has heavy vowel reduction. Look at the word "banana": you don't say BA-NA-NA. You say buh-NA-nuh (/bəˈnænə/). The unstressed "a"s shrink into that weak, lazy sound called the Schwa (/ə/).
  • In Cyrillic languages (especially Russian), vowels behave like English.

Why does this matter for Cyrillic?​

In languages with heavy vowel reduction, you cannot pronounce a word correctly just by reading its letters. You must know where the accent (stress) falls.

If you pronounce every letter purely like in Spanish, you will sound like a robot.

The Rules of Reduction​

Russian is famous for aggressive vowel reduction. There are two main types you must master:

1. Akan’ye (The O ➡️ A shift)​

This is the most important rule in Cyrillic phonetics.

  • When the letter О is STRESSED, it sounds like a pure Spanish O (oso).
  • When the letter О is UNSTRESSED, it changes completely into an A sound (or a weak English schwa).

Classic Examples:

  • Хорошо;; Bueno, bien / Good, OK

    • Stress is on the last syllable: Хорошо́
    • How it is spelled: Kh - O - r - O - sh - Ó
    • How it is pronounced: Kha-ra-shó (The first two 'O's become 'A's!)

  • Молоко;; Leche / Milk

    • Stress is on the last syllable: Молоко́
    • How it is pronounced: Ma-la-kó

  • Россия;; Rusia / Russia

    • Stress is on the 'И': Росси́я
    • How it is pronounced: Ra-ssí-ya (NOT Ro-ssi-ya)

  • Москва;; Moscú / Moscow

    • Stress is on the last syllable: Москва́
    • How it is pronounced: Mas-kvá

2. Ikan’ye (The E and Я ➡️ I shift)​

The vowels Е (ye) and Я (ya) are soft vowels. When they lose stress, they collapse toward an "I" sound (like Spanish isla).

  • When Е or Я is STRESSED, they sound like ye or ya.
  • When Е or Я is UNSTRESSED, they reduce to a weak "ee" / "ih" sound.

Classic Examples:

  • Метро;; Metro / Subway, metro

    • Stress is on the 'O': Метро́
    • The 'E' is unstressed, so it turns into an "I" sound.
    • How it is pronounced: Mi-tró (NOT Me-tro)

  • Язык;; Idioma, lengua / Language, tongue

    • Stress is on the second syllable: Язы́к
    • The 'Я' is unstressed, so it reduces toward an "I".
    • How it is pronounced: Yi-zýk (NOT Ya-zyk)

  • Телефон;; Teléfono / Telephone

    • Stress is on the last syllable: Телефо́н
    • How it is pronounced: Ti-li-fón (Both unstressed 'E's become "I"s!)

The "Immune" Vowels​

Fortunately, not all vowels reduce:

  • У (u) and Ю (yu): Stay roughly the same (/u/), stressed or not. They just become slightly shorter when unstressed.
    • Урок;; Lección, clase / Lesson, class (Stress on О; the У keeps its clear /u/ sound)
  • И (i) and Ы (hard guttural i): Stay virtually identical, just shorter.
    • Музыка;; Música / Music (Stress on У; the ы keeps its deep, guttural sound)
