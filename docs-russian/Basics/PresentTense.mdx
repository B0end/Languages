If your native language is Spanish, learning the Present Tense in Cyrillic/Slavic languages will feel remarkably natural to you.

Native English speakers suffer here because English barely conjugates verbs (*I speak, you speak, he speak**s***). But as a Spanish speaker, your brain is already hardwired for **conjugation paradigms**, verb stems, and personal endings (*yo habl**o**, tú habl**as**, él habl**a***).

---

## Surprise #1: The Verb "TO BE" Does Not Exist!

Before looking at actions, you need to know this absolute gift of Slavic grammar: **There is no present tense form of *Ser* or *Estar* (English *To Be*).**

You simply omit it:

* [Я доктор;; Yo soy doctor / I am a doctor](ЯДоктор.wav)
* [Книга здесь;; El libro está aquí / The book is here](КнигаЗдесь.wav)
* [Она умная;; Ella es inteligente / She is smart](ОнаУмная.wav)

## The Two Verb Families (Conjugations)

Just like Spanish verbs are divided into groups based on their infinitive endings (**-ar**, **-er**, **-ir**), Slavic verbs are divided into **two main groups**:

* Infinitives almost always end in **-ТЬ** (like the Spanish **-R** in *habla**r***).
* To conjugate, you cut off the **-ТЬ** and add personal endings.

---

### Group 1: The "E" Conjugation (First Conjugation)
*This is the biggest group (similar to regular **-ar** verbs in Spanish). The vowel **-Е-** dominates the endings.*

Infinitive: [Знать;; Saber, conocer / To know](Знать.wav)  
Remove the **-ть** $\rightarrow$ Stem: **Зна-**

* [Я знаю;; Yo sé, conozco / I know](ЯЗнаю.wav) *(Ending: **-ю**)*
* [Ты знаешь;; Tú sabes / You know](ТыЗнаешь.wav) *(Ending: **-ешь**)*
* [Он / Она знает;; Él / Ella sabe / He, She knows](ОнОнаЗнает.wav) *(Ending: **-ет**)*
* [Мы знаем;; Nosotros sabemos / We know](МыЗнаем.wav) *(Ending: **-ем**)*
* [Вы знаете;; Vosotros sabéis, Usted sabe / You (all) know](ВыЗнаете.wav) *(Ending: **-ете**)*
* [Они знают;; Ellos saben / They know](ОниЗнают.wav) *(Ending: **-ют**)*

Notice the pattern? Except for the first person (*Я*), **every ending has the letter E**: *-ешь, -ет, -ем, -ете*.

---

#### Group 2: The "И" Conjugation (Second Conjugation)
*Similar to Spanish **-ir** verbs. The vowel **-И-** dominates the endings.*

Infinitive: [Говорить;; Hablar / To speak](Говорить.wav)  
Remove the **-ить** $\rightarrow$ Stem: **Говор-**

* [Я говорю;; Yo hablo / I speak](ЯГоворю.wav) *(Ending: **-ю**)*
* [Ты говоришь;; Tú hablas / You speak](ТыГоворишь.wav) *(Ending: **-ишь**)*
* [Он / Она говорит;; Él / Ella habla / He, She speaks](ОнОнаГоворит.wav) *(Ending: **-ит**)*
* [Мы говорим;; Nosotros hablamos / We speak](МыГоворим.wav) *(Ending: **-им**)*
* [Вы говорите;; Vosotros habláis, Usted habla / You (all) speak](ВыГоворите.wav) *(Ending: **-ите**)*
* [Они говорят;; Ellos hablan / They speak](ОниГоворят.wav) *(Ending: **-ят**)*

Notice that here, instead of **Е**, the letter **И** dominates: *-ишь, -ит, -им, -ите*.

### Two Crucial Things Spanish Speakers Must Know

#### 1. One Present Tense for Everything (No *Estar + Gerundio*)
In English, you have: *I read* vs. *I am reading*.  
In Spanish, you have: *Yo leo* vs. *Yo estoy leyendo*.

In Slavic languages, **there is only one present tense:**
* [Я читаю;; Yo leo / Yo estoy leyendo / I read / I am reading](ЯЧитаю.wav)

You do not need to construct progressive tenses. Context tells the listener everything.

#### 2. Pronoun Dropping (Pro-Drop)
* In Spanish, you can drop pronouns: You say *“Hablo español”* without saying *“Yo”*, because the **-o** proves it's you.
* In Russian, even though the endings give away who is speaking, native speakers **usually keep the subject pronouns**:
  * [Я говорю по-русски;; (Yo) hablo ruso / I speak Russian](ЯГоворюПоРусски.wav)

### The "Catch": Verbal Aspect (Un Adelanto)

In Slavic languages, verbs come in pairs: **Imperfective** (habitual, continuous, incomplete actions) and **Perfective** (completed, one-time actions with a result).

**The Golden Rule of the Present Tense:**  
* **Only Imperfective verbs can be used in the Present Tense.**
* If you take a Perfective verb and conjugate it with the endings you learned above, **it automatically turns into the FUTURE tense.**

* [Я читаю;; Yo leo / I read (Present - Imperfective)](ЯЧитаю.wav)
* [Я прочитаю;; Yo leeré / I will read (Future - Perfective)](ЯПрочитаю.wav)

---

### Summary Checklist for the Present Tense:

1. **Delete the infinitive ending:** Knock off the **-ть**.
2. **Decide if it's Group 1 (E) or Group 2 (И):**
   * Verbs in **-ать/-ять** are usually Group 1 (**E**).
   * Verbs in **-ить** are usually Group 2 (**И**).
3. **Add the personal ending** matching your subject.
4. **Forget about "ser/estar":** Don't try to translate *am/is/are*—just skip it!
