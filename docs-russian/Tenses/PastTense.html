Past Tense

The Past Tense does NOT conjugate by person. Instead, the past tense behaves exactly like an adjective: it conjugates purely by GENDER (masculine, feminine, neuter) and NUMBER (singular vs. plural).

The Formula​

To form the past tense of almost any regular verb:

  1. Take the infinitive.
  2. Cut off the infinitive ending -ть.
  3. Add the ending that matches the subject's gender:
Subject GenderEndingFormula with Читать;; Leer / To readResulting Verb
Masculine (Él, o cualquier hombre)-лЧита- + -лОн читал;; Él leyó / He read
Feminine (Ella, o cualquier mujer)-лаЧита- + -лаОна читала;; Ella leyó / She read
Neuter (Cosas neutras: окно, радио)-лоЧита- + -лоОно читало;; Ello leyó / It read
Plural (Nosotros, vosotros, ellos, ustedes)-лиЧита- + -лиОни читали;; Ellos leyeron / They read

"I" and "You" Change With the Speaker​

Because the past tense marks gender rather than person, the words Я;; Yo / I and Ты;; Tú / You must match the physical gender of whoever is speaking or being spoken to:

If a man is speaking:​

  • Я работал вчера;; Yo trabajé ayer (hombre) / I worked yesterday (male)
  • Я не знал;; No lo sabía (hombre) / I did not know (male)

If a woman is speaking:​

  • Я работала вчера;; Yo trabajé ayer (mujer) / I worked yesterday (female)
  • Я не знала;; No lo sabía (mujer) / I did not know (female)

When asking questions:​

  • Asking a man: Ты был там?;; ¿Estuviste allí? (a un hombre) / Were you there?
  • Asking a woman: Ты была там?;; ¿Estuviste allí? (a una mujer) / Were you there?

The Return of "TO BE"​

You learned that the verb "to be" (ser / estar) does not exist in the Present Tense (Я доктор = Yo soy doctor).

However, in the past tense, "TO BE" returns completely:

  • Masculine: Он был дома;; Él estaba en casa / He was at home
  • Feminine: Она была дома;; Ella estaba en casa / She was at home
  • Neuter: Это было хорошо;; Eso fue bueno, estuvo bien / It was good
  • Plural: Мы были дома;; Estábamos en casa / We were at home
tip

When negating the feminine past of быть, the stress jumps all the way to the final vowel:

  • Она не была́;; Ella no estuvo / She was not (Pronounced "ni-by-LÁ").

The Irregulars​

Most Russian verbs are 100% regular in the past tense. However, there are two common exceptions where the pattern shifts:

1. The Disappearing -Л in Masculine Consonant Stems​

For verbs whose stems end in a consonant (like Мочь;; Poder / To be able to), the masculine form drops the -л because it is too awkward to pronounce a consonant directly next to 'л':

GenderMasculine Drops -лResult
MasculineМог (NOT Могл)Он мог;; Él pudo / He could
FeminineМог + -лаОна могла;; Ella pudo / She could
NeuterМог + -лоОно могло;; Pudo / It could
PluralМог + -лиОни могли;; Ellos pudieron / They could

Other common verbs following this rule:

  • Умереть;; Morir / To die ➡️ Он умер;; Él murió / He died vs. Она умерла;; Ella murió / She died

2. The Completely Irregular: Идти (To Go on Foot)​

Just like Spanish ir ➡️ fui, the root of Идти;; Ir a pie / To walk completely changes to ш-:

  • Masculine: Он шёл домой;; Él iba a casa a pie / He was walking home
  • Feminine: Она шла домой;; Ella iba a casa a pie / She was walking home
  • Neuter: Время шло;; El tiempo pasaba / Time was passing
  • Plural: Они шли домой;; Ellos iban a casa a pie / They were walking home

Past Tense + Aspect: The Perfect Pair​

Remember Verb Aspects? The past tense is where aspects shine most clearly for Spanish speakers:

  1. Imperfective Past (Pretérito Imperfecto - Habit or Process):
    • Я писал письмо;; Yo escribía una carta / I was writing a letter
    • Focuses on what you were doing, not if you finished.
  2. Perfective Past (Pretérito Indefinido - Completed Result):
    • Я написал письмо;; Escribí la carta (y la terminé) / I wrote the letter
    • Focuses on the finished milestone.
