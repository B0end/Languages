Future Tense

In Spanish, you choose between future tenses (leeré vs. voy a leer) mostly based on formality or immediacy.

In Cyrillic/Slavic languages, your choice of future tense is decided 100% by VERB ASPECT:

  1. Do you care about the process, duration, or habit? ➡️ Use the Compound Future (Imperfective).
  2. Do you care about the completed result or finish line? ➡️ Use the Simple Future (Perfective).

Imperfective / Process​

Use this when you want to say you will be spending time doing something, with no guarantee that you will finish it, or for actions that repeat.

Formula: Conjugated form of Быть (To Be) + Imperfective Infinitive

This works almost identically to Spanish "Voy a [infinitivo]" or English "I will be [gerund]".

The Future of "TO BE"​

This is the only auxiliary verb you ever need to conjugate in the future:

PersonAuxiliary Verb
Я (Yo)Я буду;; Yo seré, estaré, voy a / I will be
Ты (Tú)Ты будешь;; Tú serás, estarás, vas a / You will be
Он / Она / Оно (Él / Ella)Он будет;; Él será, estará, va a / He will be
Мы (Nosotros)Мы будем;; Nosotros seremos, estaremos, vamos a / We will be
Вы (Vosotros / Usted / Ustedes)Вы будете;; Vosotros seréis, Usted será / You all will be
Они (Ellos / Ellas)Они будут;; Ellos serán, estarán, van a / They will be

Slap on ANY standard imperfective infinitive​

Leave the main verb in its basic -ть form:

  • Я буду читать;; Voy a leer, estaré leyendo / I will be reading
  • Мы будем смотреть фильм;; Vamos a ver una película / We will be watching a movie
  • Они будут работать завтра;; Van a trabajar mañana / They will be working tomorrow
tip

When you say Я буду читать, you are simply describing how you will spend your time. It gives zero promise that you will actually finish the book.

Perfective / Result​

Use this when you want to promise a concrete, finished milestone or a completed result.

Formula: Perfective Verb + Present Tense Endings

There is no auxiliary word (no буду). You take the Perfective twin verb and conjugate it using the Present Tense endings.

Why does this create the future? A Perfective verb represents an action that is 100% completed and finished. Because an action cannot be already finished in the present moment while you are talking about it, putting present tense endings on a Perfective verb automatically launches the meaning into the FUTURE!

Examples:​

1. With Прочитать;; Leer por completo / To read to completion (Perfective twin of читать):​

  • Я прочитаю эту книгу;; Leeré y terminaré este libro / I will read this book (to completion)
  • Ты прочитаешь эту книгу;; Leerás y terminarás este libro / You will read this book
  • Они прочитают эту книгу;; Ellos leerán y terminarán este libro / They will read this book

2. With Сделать;; Hacer y terminar / To complete (Perfective twin of делать):​

  • Я сделаю это завтра;; Haré y terminaré esto mañana / I will get this done tomorrow
  • Мы сделаем всё вовремя;; Lo terminaremos todo a tiempo / We will finish everything on time

3. With Написать;; Escribir y terminar / To write (Perfective twin of писать):​

  • Я напишу тебе сообщение;; Te escribiré un mensaje / I will text you

Direct Side-by-Side Comparison​

Goal / ContextRussian SentenceWhat it actually tells the listener
Process / ActivityЯ буду писать письмо;; Estaré escribiendo una carta"I'll be busy writing a letter (don't know if I'll finish it)."
Result / PromiseЯ напишу письмо;; Escribiré la carta"I will write the letter from start to finish. You will get it."

Result / PromiseМы приготовим ужин;; Prepararemos la cena"Dinner will be cooked and ready on the table."

Asking Questions in the Future​

Notice how native speakers ask questions depending on what they want to know:

1. Asking about plans or how someone will spend their time (Route A):​

  • Что ты будешь делать сегодня вечером?;; ¿Qué vas a hacer esta noche? / What will you be doing tonight? (Neutral: What will you be occupying your time with?)

2. Asking about a concrete outcome or resolution (Route B):​

  • Что ты сделаешь?;; ¿Qué vas a hacer/decidir al respecto? / What will you get done? (Focus on the specific result or solution you plan to achieve).
