# Past Tense

The Past Tense does NOT conjugate by person. Instead, the past tense behaves exactly like an adjective: it conjugates purely by **GENDER** (*masculine, feminine, neuter*) and **NUMBER** (*singular vs. plural*).

---

## The Formula

To form the past tense of almost any regular verb:
1. Take the infinitive.
2. Cut off the infinitive ending **-ть**.
3. Add the ending that matches the subject's gender:

| Subject Gender | Ending | Formula with [Читать;; Leer / To read](Читать.wav) | Resulting Verb |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine** *(Él, o cualquier hombre)* | **-л** | Чита- + **-л** | [Он читал;; Él leyó / He read](ОнЧитал.wav) |
| **Feminine** *(Ella, o cualquier mujer)* | **-ла** | Чита- + **-ла** | [Она читала;; Ella leyó / She read](ОнаЧитала.wav) |
| **Neuter** *(Cosas neutras: окно, радио)* | **-ло** | Чита- + **-ло** | [Оно читало;; Ello leyó / It read](ОноЧитало.wav) |
| **Plural** *(Nosotros, vosotros, ellos, ustedes)* | **-ли** | Чита- + **-ли** | [Они читали;; Ellos leyeron / They read](ОниЧитали.wav) |

## "I" and "You" Change With the Speaker

Because the past tense marks gender rather than person, the words [Я;; Yo / I](Я.wav) and [Ты;; Tú / You](Ты.wav) must match the physical gender of whoever is speaking or being spoken to:

### If a man is speaking:
* [Я работал вчера;; Yo trabajé ayer (hombre) / I worked yesterday (male)](ЯРаботалВчера.wav)
* [Я не знал;; No lo sabía (hombre) / I did not know (male)](ЯНеЗнал.wav)

### If a woman is speaking:
* [Я работала вчера;; Yo trabajé ayer (mujer) / I worked yesterday (female)](ЯРаботалаВчера.wav)
* [Я не знала;; No lo sabía (mujer) / I did not know (female)](ЯНеЗнала.wav)

### When asking questions:
* Asking a man: [Ты был там?;; ¿Estuviste allí? (a un hombre) / Were you there?](ТыБылТам.wav)
* Asking a woman: [Ты была там?;; ¿Estuviste allí? (a una mujer) / Were you there?](ТыБылаТам.wav)

## The Return of "TO BE"

You learned that the verb "to be" (*ser / estar*) does not exist in the Present Tense (Я доктор = Yo soy doctor).

However, **in the past tense, "TO BE" returns completely:**

* Masculine: [Он был дома;; Él estaba en casa / He was at home](ОнБылДома.wav)
* Feminine: [Она была дома;; Ella estaba en casa / She was at home](ОнаБылаДома.wav)
* Neuter: [Это было хорошо;; Eso fue bueno, estuvo bien / It was good](ЭтоБылоХорошо.wav)
* Plural: [Мы были дома;; Estábamos en casa / We were at home](МыБылиДома.wav)

:::tip
When negating the feminine past of быть, the stress jumps all the way to the final vowel:  
* [Она не была́;; Ella no estuvo / She was not](ОнаНеБыла.wav) *(Pronounced "ni-by-LÁ")*.
:::

## The Irregulars

Most Russian verbs are 100% regular in the past tense. However, there are two common exceptions where the pattern shifts:

### 1. The Disappearing -Л in Masculine Consonant Stems
For verbs whose stems end in a consonant (like [Мочь;; Poder / To be able to](Мочь.wav)), the masculine form **drops the -л** because it is too awkward to pronounce a consonant directly next to 'л':

| Gender | Masculine Drops -л | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Masculine** | Мог (NOT Могл) | [Он мог;; Él pudo / He could](ОнМог.wav) |
| **Feminine** | Мог + **-ла** | [Она могла;; Ella pudo / She could](ОнаМогла.wav) |
| **Neuter** | Мог + **-ло** | [Оно могло;; Pudo / It could](ОноМогло.wav) |
| **Plural** | Мог + **-ли** | [Они могли;; Ellos pudieron / They could](ОниМогли.wav) |

Other common verbs following this rule:
* [Умереть;; Morir / To die](Умереть.wav) ➡️ [Он умер;; Él murió / He died](ОнУмер.wav) vs. [Она умерла;; Ella murió / She died](ОнаУмерла.wav)

---

### 2. The Completely Irregular: Идти (To Go on Foot)
Just like Spanish *ir ➡️ fui*, the root of [Идти;; Ir a pie / To walk](Идти.wav) completely changes to **ш-**:

* Masculine: [Он шёл домой;; Él iba a casa a pie / He was walking home](ОнШёлДомой.wav)
* Feminine: [Она шла домой;; Ella iba a casa a pie / She was walking home](ОнаШлаДомой.wav)
* Neuter: [Время шло;; El tiempo pasaba / Time was passing](ВремяШло.wav)
* Plural: [Они шли домой;; Ellos iban a casa a pie / They were walking home](ОниШлиДомой.wav)

## Past Tense + Aspect: The Perfect Pair

Remember Verb Aspects? The past tense is where aspects shine most clearly for Spanish speakers:

1. **Imperfective Past (Pretérito Imperfecto - Habit or Process):**
   * [Я писал письмо;; Yo escribía una carta / I was writing a letter](ЯПисалПисьмо.wav)
   * Focuses on what you were doing, not if you finished.
2. **Perfective Past (Pretérito Indefinido - Completed Result):**
   * [Я написал письмо;; Escribí la carta (y la terminé) / I wrote the letter](ЯНаписалПисьмо.wav)
   * Focuses on the finished milestone.
