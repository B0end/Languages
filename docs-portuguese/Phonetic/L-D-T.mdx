# Syllable-Final "L" and the "D" & "T" Transformation

Two of the fastest ways to eliminate a Spanish accent in Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR) are:
1. Turning the **syllable-final "L"** into a **"W"**.
2. Palatalizing **"D"** and **"T"** into **"DJ"** and **"CH"** before an `/i/` sound.

---

## 1. Syllable-Final "L" → The "W" Sound

In Spanish, an **L** at the end of a syllable is an alveolar consonant: your tongue tip firmly taps the roof of your mouth (*al-to*, *pa-pel*, *sol*).

In Brazilian Portuguese, whenever **L** appears at the end of a syllable or word, the tongue **never touches the roof of the mouth**. Your lips round into a semi-vowel **`/w/`** (like the *u* in *auto*).

:::tip
The "Mal" vs. "Mau" Secret

Because final **L** sounds like **W**, the words **mal** *(badly)* and **mau** *(bad)* sound **100% identical** in spoken Brazilian Portuguese: both are pronounced `"maw"`.
:::

## 2. The "D" and "T" Transformation

In Spanish, **D** and **T** are always dental: your tongue hits the back of your upper front teeth (*tío*, *diente*).

In most of Brazil, whenever **D** or **T** is followed by an **`/i/` sound**, they transform into affricates:
* **T + [i]** → Pronounced like the English **"CH"** in *chocolate* (`/tʃ/`).
* **D + [i]** → Pronounced like the English **"J"** or **"DJ"** in *jeep* (`/dʒ/`).

### When Does This Happen?
This shift occurs in two situations:
1. When written with the letter **I** (e.g., *dia*, *tia*).
2. When written with an unstressed final **E** (because final unstressed *e* reduces to an `/i/` sound: *noite* → `"noi-chi"`).
