Nasal Vowels

In Spanish, all vowels are oral (100% of the air exits through the mouth). Even with words like pan or con, the vowel itself remains oral until the tongue physically touches the teeth or palate to make the consonant.

In Portuguese, nasal vowels occur when the soft palate drops, splitting the airflow: ~50% through the mouth and ~50% through the nose during the vowel.

tip

The Golden Rule of M & N

At the end of a syllable, M and N are not consonants; they are diacritics (nasal markers).

  • Spanish: con → The tongue hits the palate: C - O - [N]
  • Portuguese: com → The tongue stays flat and lips do not touch: C - [Õ]

4. Daily Diagnostic Drill​

Use this sentence to test your airflow continuity. Hold two fingers on the bridge of your nose; you should feel a continuous vibration across nearly every word:

O homem não come pão com maçã de manhã.
(The man does not eat bread with apple in the morning.)

Phonetic Breakdown:​

O ho-m[ẽy] n[ãw] come p[ãw] c[õ] ma-s[ã] de ma-nh[ã]
