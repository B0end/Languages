# Open vs. Closed Vowels

In **Spanish**, you have exactly **5 pure vowel sounds** ($a, e, i, o, u$). They are stable, medium-open, and never change height.

In **Portuguese**, there are **7 distinct oral vowel sounds**. The vowels **E** and **O** each split into two completely different phonemes: an **open sound** and a **closed sound**. Swapping them will change the meaning of a word or immediately reveal a heavy "Portuñol" accent.

:::tip
The Accent Mark Cheat Code

Brazilian orthography explicitly tells you how to pronounce stressed vowels:
* **Acute accent (`´`) = OPEN vowel:** The jaw drops wider (e.g., *é*, *ó*).
* **Circumflex accent (`^`) = CLOSED vowel:** The mouth is more closed/rounded (e.g., *ê*, *ô*).
:::

## 3. The "Ovo Rule" (Metaphony in Plurals)

A classic trap for Spanish speakers: many masculine nouns with a **closed O** (`/o/`) in the singular shift to an **open O** (`/ɔ/`) in the plural.

## 4. Unstressed Final Vowel Reduction

In Spanish, final vowels remain pure: *tomat**e*** ends with an energetic [e], and *libr**o*** ends with an energetic [o].

In Brazilian Portuguese, **unstressed vowels at the end of a word reduce**:
* Final unstressed **-E** becomes **`/i/`** (written as *e*, pronounced as *i*).
* Final unstressed **-O** becomes **`/u/`** (written as *o*, pronounced as *u*).

| Word | Written | Spoken (PT-BR) | Spanish Mistake to Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [Noite; Night](/static/audios-PT/noite.wav) | *noi-te* | **"noi-chi"** | Saying "noi-te" with a hard Spanish "te" |
| [Leite; Milk](/static/audios-PT/leite.wav) | *lei-te* | **"lei-chi"** | Saying "lei-te" with a crisp final "e" |
| [Carro; Car](/static/audios-PT/carro.wav) | *car-ro* | **"ca-hu"** | Saying "ca-rro" with a rolled R and crisp "o" |
| [Livro; Book](/static/audios-PT/livro.wav) | *li-vro* | **"li-vru"** | Finishing with a strong Spanish "o" |

## 5. Daily Diagnostic Drill

Read this sentence aloud to contrast open and closed vowels back-to-back:

> [A minha avó tem sede, mas o meu avô bebe café na sede da empresa.](/static/audios-PT/frase-diagnostico-vogais.wav)  
> *(My grandmother is thirsty, but my grandfather drinks coffee at the company headquarters.)*

### Phonetic Breakdown:
* **avó** → Open `/ɔ/` *(jaw dropped)*
* **sede** (thirst) → Open `/ɛ/` *(jaw dropped)*
* **avô** → Closed `/o/` *(rounded lips)*
* **café** → Open `/ɛ/` *(jaw dropped)*
* **sede** (headquarters) → Closed `/e/` *(lips tense)*
