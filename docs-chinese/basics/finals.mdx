# Finals

Now that you know the Initials (consonants), let’s look at the other half of the syllable: **The Finals**.

The Final is the **rest of the syllable** after the initial consonant. It is built around vowels and can sometimes end with a nasal consonant coda (**-n** or **-ng**).

---

## 1. The 6 Basic Simple Finals

| Final | IPA | Mouth Shape & Articulation | Example | Meaning |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **a** | [a] | Open mouth wide; tongue rests low and flat. | [啊 ā](a1.wav) | Ah! *(exclamation)* |
| **o** | [o] | Lips rounded into an "O" shape; tongue pulled back. | [喔 ō](o1.wav) | Oh! *(rooster crow)* |
| **e** | [ɤ] | Keep tongue in the **"o"** position, but pull lips back flat into an unrounded smile. | [鹅 é](e2.wav) | Goose |
| **i** | [i] | Spread lips into a wide, flat smile; tongue raised high and forward. | [衣 yī](yi1.wav) | Clothes |
| **u** | [u] | Purse lips tightly into a small forward circle; tongue pulled back high. | [屋 wū](wu1.wav) | House, room |
| **ü** | [y] | Keep tongue in the high **"i"** position, but purse lips into a tight circle like **"u"**. | [鱼 yú](yu2.wav) | Fish |

---

> 💡 **Remember the Standalone Rules from Group 6:**
> * **`i`** cannot stand alone ➔ written as **`yi`** ([衣 yī](yi1.wav))
> * **`u`** cannot stand alone ➔ written as **`wu`** ([屋 wū](wu1.wav))
> * **`ü`** cannot stand alone ➔ written as **`yu`** with dots dropped ([鱼 yú](yu2.wav))

## 2. Compound Finals

Compound finals are diphthongs and triphthongs: you start at the first vowel and glide smoothly into the next.

> 💡 **Notice the "e" shift:** When the letter **e** is part of a front blend (like *ie, üe, ei*), it shifts from the throaty *"uh"* sound to a crisp *"eh"* or *"ay"*.

| Category | Final | Sound Guide / Glide | Example | Meaning |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Basic Diphthongs** | **ai** | Glides "a" ➔ "i" *(like English eye)* | [白 bái](bai2.wav) | White |
| | **ei** | Glides "e" ➔ "i" *(like English bay)* | [杯 bēi](bei1.wav) | Cup, glass |
| | **ao** | Glides "a" ➔ "o" *(like English cow)* | [高 gāo](gao1.mp3) | Tall, high |
| | **ou** | Glides "o" ➔ "u" *(like English so)* | [口 kǒu](kou3.wav) | Mouth |
| **Rising Glides** <br />*(Medials i / u / ü)* | **ia** | "ee" + "ah" | [家 jiā](jia1.mp3) | Home, family |
| | **ie** | "ee" + "eh" *(like yes)* | [谢 xiè](xie4.wav) | Thanks |
| | **ua** | "oo" + "ah" | [花 huā](hua1.mp3) | Flower |
| | **uo** | "oo" + "aw" | [多 duō](duo1.mp3) | Many, much |
| | **üe** | "ü" + "eh" | [月 yuè](yue4.wav) | Moon, month |
| **Triphthongs & Shortcuts** | **iao** | "ee" + "ow" | [条 tiáo](tiao2.wav) | Strip, item |
| | **iu** | "ee" + "oh" + "oo" *(spelling shortcut for **-iou**)* | [九 jiǔ](jiu3.wav) | Nine |
| | **uai** | "oo" + "eye" | [快 kuài](kuai4.wav) | Fast, quick |
| | **ui** | "oo" + "ay" *(spelling shortcut for **-uei**)* | [水 shuǐ](shui3.wav) | Water |
| **Special Rhotic** | **er** | Curled-tongue vowel *(never pairs with an initial)* | [二 èr](er4.wav) | Two |

## 3. Nasal Ending Finals (`-n` vs. `-ng`)

> **The Mechanics:**
> * **Front Nasal (`-n`):** The tongue tip presses against the gum ridge behind your **front upper teeth** (just like an English *n* in *sun*).
> * **Back Nasal (`-ng`):** The back of the tongue rises to seal against the **soft palate** in the back of the mouth, with no front-teeth contact (like *ng* in *song*).

---

| **e** | **en** | [人 rén](ren2.wav) | Person | **eng** | [冷 lěng](leng3.wav) | Cold |
| **i** | **in** | [金 jīn](jin1.wav) | Gold, metal | **ing** | [星 xīng](xing1.wav) | Star |
| **u / o** | **un** | [轮 lún](lun2.wav) | Wheel | **ong** | [中 zhōng](zhong1.wav) | Middle |
| **ia** | **ian** | [电 diàn](dian4.wav) | Electricity | **iang** | [亮 liàng](liang4.wav) | Bright |
| **ua** | **uan** | [关 guān](guan1.wav) | To close | **uang** | [光 guāng](guang1.mp3) | Light |

---

### ⚠️ Two Critical Pronunciation Traps

#### 1. The `-ian` and `-üan` Vowel Shift ("a" sounds like "e")
In standard **`-an`**, the letter **`a`** is an open *"ah"* sound (*like English pan*).  
However, whenever **`a`** is squeezed after **`i`** or **`ü`**, it automatically shifts into a crisp **"eh"** sound (*like English pen*):
* [天 tiān](tian1.wav) ➡️ pronounced **"tee-en"** *(rhymes with pen, not pan)*
* [全 quán](quan2.wav) ➡️ pronounced **"ch-ü-en"** *(rhymes with ü + pen)*

#### 2. The `-uan` Spelling Trap: Real `u` vs. Secret `ü`
Because **j, q, x,** and **y** cause the umlaut dots on **ü** to be dropped, the written letters **`-uan`** represent two completely different sounds depending on the initial consonant:

| Written Form | Initials | Actual Final | Sound / Vowel | Examples |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **-uan** | Standard consonants:<br />*(d, t, n, l, g, k, h, z, c, s, zh, ch, sh, r)* | **Real `-uan`** | **"oo-ahn"** <br />*(Open "ah")* | • [乱 luàn](luan4.wav) – Messy<br />• [短 duǎn](duan3.wav) – Short<br />• [换 huàn](huan4.wav) – To change |
| **-uan** | **j, q, x, y** <br />*(Dots dropped!)* | **Hidden `-üan`** | **"ü-en"** <br />*(Shifted "eh")* | • [卷 juǎn](juan3.wav) – Roll / curl<br />• [选 xuǎn](xuan3.wav) – To choose<br />• [院 yuàn](yuan4.wav) – Courtyard |

## 4. The "`ü`" Dot Rule (When to Drop vs. When to Keep)

> 🧠 **The Linguistic Logic:**
> * **`j, q, x, y`:** Physically **cannot** pair with the regular **`u`** (*"oo"*) sound in Mandarin. Because there is zero risk of confusion, Pinyin **drops the dots** (`ju`, `qu`, `xu`, `yu`).
> * **`n` and `l`:** Can pair with **both** regular **`u`** and umlaut **`ü`**. Therefore, the dots **must be kept** to distinguish completely different words (`nu` vs. `nü`, `lu` vs. `lü`).

| Initial | Written Pinyin | Actual Sound | The Dot Rule | Example | Meaning |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **j** | **ju** | *j + ü* | ❌ **Drop dots** | [句 jù](ju4.wav) | Sentence |
| **q** | **qu** | *q + ü* | ❌ **Drop dots** | [去 qù](qu4.wav) | To go |
| **x** | **xu** | *x + ü* | ❌ **Drop dots** | [需 xū](xu1.wav) | To need |
| **y** | **yu** | *ü* | ❌ **Drop dots** | [雨 yǔ](yu3.wav) | Rain |
| **n** | **nü** | *n + ü* | ✅ **Keep dots** | [女 nǚ](nu3.wav) | Woman |
| **l** | **lü** | *l + ü* | ✅ **Keep dots** | [绿 lǜ](lü4.wav) | Green |

---

### Why N and L Need the Dots (Minimal Pair Contrast)

If the dots were dropped for **n** and **l**, you would not be able to tell these words apart:

| Initial | Regular `u` <br /> *(Standard "oo" sound)* | Meaning | Umlaut `ü` <br /> *(Whistling "ee" sound)* | Meaning |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **n** | [怒 nù](nu4.wav) | Angry | [女 nǚ](nu3.wav) | Woman |
| **l** | [路 lù](lu4.wav) | Road | [绿 lǜ](lü4.wav) | Green |

---

> 💡 **Bonus Typing Trick:**  
> Standard keyboards do not have a **ü** key. To type **女** (*nǚ*) or **绿** (*lǜ*), use the letter **`v`**:  
> * Type `nv` ➔ **女**  
> * Type `lv` ➔ **绿**

## 5. All Sounds of Pinyin "E"

> * **By itself:** Say **"UH"** *(deep/throaty)* ➔ [哥 gē](ge1.mp3), [喝 hē](he1.wav), [的 de](de.wav)
> * **In a front blend:** Say **"EH"** or **"AY"** ➔ [谢 xiè](xie4xie.wav), [月 yuè](yue4.wav), [北 běi](bei3.wav)
> * **Followed by "r":** Say **"ERR"** *(tongue curled)* ➔ [二 èr](er4.wav)

## 6. The "Hidden E" Finals (Spelling Abbreviation Rules)

> In Pinyin orthography, the letter **`e`** is omitted in spelling whenever an initial consonant is added, but the vowel sound remains fully pronounced.

### The Two "Hidden E" Rules

| Base Final | Written Form | Pronunciation Guide | Standalone <br />*(Zero Initial)* |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :---: |
| **-uei** | **-ui** | Starts with **"u"**, glides through **"eh"**, and ends in **"i"** *(sounds like English "way")* | [为 wèi](wei4.wav) |
| **-uen** | **-un** | Starts with **"u"**, glides through a short neutral **"uh"**, and ends in nasal **"n"** *(sounds like English "won")* | [问 wèn](wen4.wav) |

### All Valid Syllable Combinations

Because **`-ui`** and **`-un`** pair with almost the exact same initials, here is the complete side-by-side comparison of every valid syllable:
