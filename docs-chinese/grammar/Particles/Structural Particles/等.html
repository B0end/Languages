等 (děng)

In Mandarin Chinese, 等 (děng) is a versatile character with three primary grammatical functions:

  1. A Verb: "To wait"
  2. A Conjunction: "When...", "Once...", or "By the time..."
  3. A Particle/Suffix: "Etc." or "And so on"

Here is a detailed breakdown of how to use 等 in each context.

1. As a Verb: "To Wait"​

This is the most basic and common usage.

A. Basic Transitive Verb (Wait for someone/something)​

Structure: Subject + 等 + Person / Thing

  • 请等我一下。
    Qǐng děng wǒ yíxià.
    Please wait for me a moment.
  • 你在等谁？
    Nǐ zài děng shéi?
    Who are you waiting for?
  • 我在等公交车。
    Wǒ zài děng gōngjiāochē.
    I am waiting for the bus.

B. Waiting for Someone to Do Something​

Structure: 等 + Person + Verb Phrase

  • 我等你下班。
    Wǒ děng nǐ xiàbān.
    I’ll wait for you to get off work.
  • 我们在等他做决定。
    Wǒmen zài děng tā zuò juédìng.
    We are waiting for him to make a decision.

C. Common Verb Phrases:​

  • 等一下 (děng yíxià) / 等会儿 (děng huìr): "Wait a moment."
  • 等等 (děngděng): When used as an imperative verb at the beginning of a sentence, it means "Hold on!" or "Wait a second!"
    • 等等！你忘了带钥匙。 (Wait! You forgot your keys.)
  • 等不及 (děng bu jí): "Can't wait" (too excited/impatient).
    • 我等不及要看这部电影了！ (I can't wait to see this movie!)

2. As a Conjunction: "When...", "Once...", "By the time..."​

When introducing a clause, 等 often sets up a future condition or time point. It implies waiting for a certain point in time before the next action takes place.

Structure: 等 + (Event/Time) + (的时候), ... 就 / 再 + (Main Action)

  • 等我到了，就给你打电话。
    Děng wǒ dào le, jiù gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.
    When/Once I arrive, I will call you.
  • 等天气好了，我们再去公园吧。
    Děng tiānqì hǎo le, wǒmen zài qù gōngyuán ba.
    Once the weather gets better, then let's go to the park.
  • 等你有空的时候，我们聊聊。
    Děng nǐ yǒu kòng de shíhou, wǒmen liáoliao.
    When you have free time, let's chat.

Variation: 等到 (děngdào) - "Waiting until..."​

Adding 到 (dào) emphasizes the duration or a specific endpoint.

  • 等到明天再说吧。
    Děngdào míngtiān zài shuō ba.
    Let's wait until tomorrow to discuss it.
  • 等到他来的时候，派对已经结束了。
    Děngdào tā lái de shíhou, pàiduì yǐjīng jiéshù le.
    By the time he arrived, the party had already ended.

3. As a Particle: "Etc." / "And so on"​

Used at the end of a list to indicate that other items exist. You can use either a single 等 or reduplicated 等等.

A. 等等 (děngděng) — Informal / Spoken / Written​

Functions exactly like the English "etc." Structure: Noun 1, Noun 2, Noun 3 + 等等

  • 我买了苹果、香蕉、橙子等等。
    Wǒ mǎile píngguǒ, xiāngjiāo, chéngzi děngděng.
    I bought apples, bananas, oranges, etc.

B. 等 (děng) — Formal / Written​

In formal or written Chinese, a single 等 is often followed by a category noun that summarizes the list.

Structure: Noun 1, Noun 2 + 等 + Category Noun

  • 北京、上海、广州等大城市。
    Běijīng, Shànghǎi, Guǎngzhōu děng dà chéngshì.
    Big cities such as Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, etc.
  • 英语、法语、汉语等语言。
    Yīngyǔ, Fǎyǔ, Hànyǔ děng yǔyán.
    Languages such as English, French, Chinese, etc.
