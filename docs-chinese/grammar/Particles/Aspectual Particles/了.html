了 (le)

Completion of an action (when used right after a verb: Verb + 了)

Instead of marking time (past/present/future), 了 does two distinct jobs:

  1. Verb + 了 : Marks the completion / realization of an action.
  2. Sentence-Final 了: Marks a change of state / new situation ("now" / "anymore").
TypePositionFunctionMental Shortcut
了 (Aspect)Directly after VerbAction is completed / finished"Done!"
了 (Modal)At the end of sentenceA new situation / state change occurred"Things have changed / Now"
Double 了Both after verb AND at the endAction completed up to now AND still continuing"Have been doing for... and still am"

Verb + 了: Completed Action (Aspect Marker)​

When placed directly after a verb, 了 functions as an aspect marker indicating that an action is completed or realized.

info

了 le is Not Past Tense. It indicates completion of an action, not grammatical past tense. It can be used for actions completed in the past, actions happening routinely, or actions that will be completed in the future before another action begins.

When using verbal 了 with a direct object, the object normally takes a specific modifier (such as a quantity, measure word, or descriptive adjective) so the sentence does not sound abruptly unfinished.

Here is the updated table with all three affirmative examples consolidated into a single row:

Sentence FormChinese PatternExample
AffirmativeSubj + Verb + 了 le + Quantity / Modifier + Obj我买了一本书。 Wǒ mǎile yì běn shū.
⤷ I bought a book.

他喝了两杯咖啡。 Tā hēle liǎng bēi kāfēi.
⤷ He drank two cups of coffee.

妈妈吃了一个红苹果。 Māma chīle yí ge hóng píngguǒ.
⤷ Mom ate a red apple.
NegativeSubj + 没 méi / 没有 méiyǒu + Verb + Obj我没买书。 Wǒ méi mǎi shū.
⤷ I didn't buy books.
Yes/No QuestionSubj + Verb + Obj + 了吗 le ma? (or 没有 méiyǒu?)你喝咖啡了吗？ Nǐ hē kāfēi le ma?
⤷ Did you drink coffee?
A-not-A QuestionSubj + 有没有 yǒu méiyǒu + Verb + Obj？你有没有喝咖啡？ Nǐ yǒu méiyǒu hē kāfēi?
⤷ Did you drink coffee (or not)?

Consecutive Actions ("As Soon As... Then...")​

When two events occur sequentially, attach 了 directly to the first verb to show that Action 1 must conclude before Action 2 starts.

note

Subject + Verb 1 + 了 + (Obj 1) + 就 + Verb 2 + (Obj 2)

Time FrameStructureExample
Future / PlannedVerb 1 + 了 le ... 就 jiù + Verb 2我到了家就给你打电话。 Wǒ dàole jiā jiù gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.
⤷ As soon as I arrive home, I will call you.
Sequential FlowVerb 1 + 了 le ... + Verb 2我们吃了晚饭去看电影。 Wǒmen chīle wǎnfàn qù kàn diànyǐng.
⤷ After eating dinner, we will go watch a movie.
Past CompletedVerb 1 + 了 le ... 就 jiù + Verb 2他下了班就回家了。 Tā xiàle bān jiù huí jiā le.
⤷ As soon as he got off work, he went home.

Sentence-Final 了: Change of State​

When 了 appears at the very end of a sentence, it signals that the situation has changed. It was not like this before, but it is now.

note

[Statement / Condition] + 了 (le)
(Implies: "...now" or "...anymore")

State BeforeSentence with 了₂Meaning
It was dry下雨了！ Xiàyǔ le!It has started raining! (It wasn't raining before).
I didn't know我知道了。 Wǒ zhīdào le.I know now / I understand now.
He was single他有女朋友了。 Tā yǒu nǚpéngyou le.He has a girlfriend now.
I used to eat meat我不吃肉了。 Wǒ bù chī ròu le.I don't eat meat anymore.
You were small你长大了！ Nǐ zhǎngdà le!You've grown up!

💡 Notice the Negation Difference:

  • 我不去。 Wǒ bú qù. = I am not going (simple refusal).
  • 我不去了。 Wǒ bú qù le. = I'm not going anymore (I originally planned to go, but changed my mind).

Double 了: "Present Perfect Continuous"​

What happens when you put 了 after the verb AND at the end of the sentence?
It means the action started in the past, completed up to this moment, and is still ongoing right now.

note

Subject + Verb + 了 + [Duration] + Object + 了

Compare Single 了 vs. Double 了​

StructureChinese SentenceWhat it Really Means
Single 了
(Finished)		我学了一年中文。 Wǒ xuéle yì nián Zhōngwén.I studied Chinese for one year. (I'm not studying anymore).
Double 了
(Still ongoing)		我学了一年中文了。 Wǒ xuéle yì nián Zhōngwén le.I have been studying Chinese for a year (and I am still studying it).
Single 了
(Finished)		他睡了八个小时。 Tā shuìle bā gè xiǎoshí.He slept for 8 hours. (He is awake now).
Double 了
(Still ongoing)		他睡了八个小时了。 Tā shuìle bā gè xiǎoshí le.He has been asleep for 8 hours (and is still sleeping).

Fixed Common Patterns with 了​

In everyday Mandarin, 了 appears in several high-frequency fixed grammatical structures that go beyond simple past completion.

PatternMeaning / FunctionExamples
快 kuài ... 了 le
要 yào ... 了 le
快要 kuàiyào ... 了 le		Imminent Action
⤷ Something is about to occur in the very near future.		快下雨了！ Kuài xiàyǔ le!
⤷ It's about to rain!

火车要开了。 Huǒchē yào kāi le.
⤷ The train is about to leave.

新年快要到了。 Xīnnián kuàiyào dào le.
⤷ New Year is almost here.
太 tài + Adj / Verb + 了 leExtreme Degree
⤷ Expresses strong emotion, approval, or exceeding a limit ("too / extremely").		太好了！ Tài hǎo le!
⤷ Great! / Wonderful!

太贵了！ Tài guì le!
⤷ Too expensive!

太感谢你了！ Tài gǎnxiè nǐ le!
⤷ Thank you so much!
别 bié / 不要 bú yào + Verb + 了 leCeasing an Action
⤷ Demands stopping an ongoing activity (signals a change of state: "stop doing that!").		别说了！ Bié shuō le!
⤷ Stop talking!

别哭了！ Bié kū le!
⤷ Stop crying!

不要想了！ Bú yào xiǎng le!
⤷ Stop overthinking it!

When you state a specific time point (e.g., tomorrow, next week, at 3:00), you must use 就要...了 instead of 快 or 快要:

❌ 明天天气快要下雨了。 Míngtiān tiānqì kuàiyào xiàyǔ le. (Cannot use specific time with 快要)

✔️ 明天我们就要出发了。 Míngtiān wǒmen jiùyào chūfā le. We are setting off tomorrow.

了 (liǎo) as a Verb and Potential Complement​

When pronounced liǎo instead of neutral le, this character retains its original lexical meaning: "to finish", "to resolve", or "to be able to".

Usage TypeExpression / PatternExample Sentence
Standard Vocabulary了解 liǎojiě
⤷ To understand / comprehend		我很了解他。 Wǒ hěn liǎojiě tā.
⤷ I understand him very well.
Idiomatic Expression受不了 shòubuliǎo
⤷ Cannot stand / cannot endure		我受不了这个天气。 Wǒ shòubuliǎo zhège tiānqì.
⤷ I cannot stand this weather.
Extreme Degree不得了 bùdéliǎo
⤷ Terrific / extremely serious		今天热得不得了。 Jīntiān rè de bùdéliǎo.
⤷ Today is unbelievably hot.
Potential ComplementVerb + 得了 deliǎo / 不了 buliǎo
⤷ Able / unable to finish an action		这么多菜，你吃得了吃不了？ Zhèmeduō cài, nǐ chī deliǎo chī buliǎo?
⤷ With this much food, can you finish it or not?
