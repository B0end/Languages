过 (guò / guo)

过 functions in two distinct ways in Mandarin:

  1. As an Aspect Particle (guo, neutral tone): Attached to a verb to indicate past life experience ("have done before").
  2. As a Full Verb (guò, 4th tone): Means to cross, pass by, or celebrate/spend time.

1. Experiential Particle: Verb + 过​

When placed directly after a verb, 过 (guo) indicates that an action has occurred at least once in the past. It highlights personal experience rather than the immediate completion of a task.

Core Sentence Patterns​

Sentence FormChinese PatternExample Sentence
AffirmativeSubj + Verb + 过 + Object我去过中国。 Wǒ qùguo Zhōngguó.
⤷ I have been to China before.

他吃过北京烤鸭。 Tā chīguo Běijīng kǎoyā.
⤷ He has eaten Peking duck before.

我看过这部电影。 Wǒ kànguo zhè bù diànyǐng.
⤷ I have seen this movie before.
NegativeSubj + 没(有) + Verb + 过 + Object我没吃过臭豆腐。 Wǒ méi chīguo chòudòufu.
⤷ I have never eaten stinky tofu.

他没有去过日本。 Tā méiyǒu qùguo Rìběn.
⤷ He has never been to Japan.
Emphatic NeverSubj + 从来没 + Verb + 过 + Object我从来没学过法语。 Wǒ cónglái méi xuéguo Fǎyǔ.
⤷ I have never studied French in my life.
Question (吗)Subj + Verb + 过 + Object + 吗？你喝过白酒吗？ Nǐ hēguo báijiǔ ma?
⤷ Have you ever drunk Baijiu?
Question (没有)Subj + Verb + 过 + Object + 没有？你看过这本书没有？ Nǐ kànguo zhè běn shū méiyǒu?
⤷ Have you read this book (or not)?
Question (有没有)Subj + 有没有 + Verb + 过 + Object？你有没有去过北京？ Nǐ yǒu méiyǒu qùguo Běijīng?
⤷ Have you ever been to Beijing?

2. 过 (guo) vs. 了 (le)​

  • 过 (guo) = An experience that is over and in the past. Focuses on whether it has ever happened.
  • 了 (le) = A completed action connected to a specific event or current state.
ContextSentence with 过Sentence with 了
Food你吃过吗？ Nǐ chīguo ma?
(Have you ever tasted this food in your life?)		你吃了吗？ Nǐ chīle ma?
(Did you eat lunch/dinner today?)
Travel他去过中国。 Tā qùguo Zhōngguó.
(He has visited China before — he is back home now).		他去了中国。 Tā qùle Zhōngguó.
(He went to China — he is likely still there).
Illness我感冒过。 Wǒ gǎnmàoguo.
(I have had colds before in my life).		我感冒了。 Wǒ gǎnmào le.
(I caught a cold — I am sick right now).

3. Main Verb: To Cross, Pass, or Celebrate (guò)​

When pronounced in the 4th tone (guò), 过 acts as a full lexical verb rather than an aspect particle. Its meanings fall into three primary categories:

CategoryCommon CollocationsExample Sentence
Crossing Physical Space过马路 (cross street)
过桥 (cross bridge)
过河 (cross river)		过马路的时候要小心。 Guò mǎlù de shíhou yào xiǎoxīn.
⤷ Be careful when crossing the street.
Spending / Celebrating Time过生日 (celebrate birthday)
过年 (celebrate New Year)
过周末 (spend weekend)		祝你过得愉快！ Zhù nǐ guò de yúkuài!
⤷ Have a pleasant time!
Passing Beyond a Threshold过十二点 (past 12 o'clock)
过四十岁 (over 40 years old)
过期 (expire / pass limit)		现在过了十二点。 Xiànzài guòle shí'èr diǎn.
⤷ It is now past 12 o'clock.

这盒牛奶过期了。 Zhè hé niúnǎi guòqī le.
⤷ This box of milk has expired.

4. Directional Complements: 过来 (guòlái) vs. 过去 (guòqù)​

Attach 过来 or 过去 after a movement verb to indicate the direction of an action crossing space relative to the speaker. They also express metaphorical shifts in consciousness or state:

ComplementPhysical DirectionMetaphorical StateExample Sentence
Verb + 过来Toward the speaker
⤷ "Over here"		Returning to normal / clarity
⤷ Regaining consciousness, fixing an error		你过来一下！ Nǐ guòlái yíxià!
⤷ Come over here for a second!

病人醒过来了。 Bìngrén xǐng guòlái le.
⤷ The patient regained consciousness.
Verb + 过去Away from the speaker
⤷ "Over there"		Losing normal state / fading
⤷ Passing out, fainting, drifting off		请走过去。 Qǐng zǒu guòqù.
⤷ Please walk over there.

他晕过去了。 Tā yūn guòqù le.
⤷ He fainted / passed out.

WordMeaningExample Sentence
