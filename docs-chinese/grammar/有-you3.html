有 (yǒu)

1. Possession: "To Have / Own"​

In possession sentences, 有 directly links the possessor (subject) to the object owned.

warning

The Golden Rule: Never Say "不有"

In Mandarin, virtually all verbs are negated with 不 (bù). 有 is the absolute exception.
You must always negate 有 with 没 (méi) or 没有 (méiyǒu).

  • ❌ 我不有钱 (Incorrect)
  • ✔️ 我没有钱 (Correct)

Sentence Patterns for Possession​

Sentence FormChinese PatternExample Sentence
AffirmativeSubj + 有 + Object我有一只猫。; Wǒ yǒu yì zhī māo.; I have a cat.

我们有时间。; Wǒmen yǒu shíjiān.; We have time.
NegativeSubj + 没有 / 没 + Object我没有钱。; Wǒ méiyǒu qián.; I don't have money.

他没车。; Tā méi chē.; He doesn't have a car (colloquial short form).
Question (吗)Subj + 有 + Object + 吗？你有铅笔吗？; Nǐ yǒu qiānbǐ ma?; Do you have a pencil?
Question (A-not-A)Subj + 有没有 + Object？你有没有铅笔？; Nǐ yǒu méiyǒu qiānbǐ?; Do you have a pencil (or not)?

2. Existence: "There Is / There Are"​

English uses the dummy subject "there", but Mandarin has no dummy pronouns. Instead, the location itself serves as the subject, followed by 有:

Formula: Location / Place + 有 (yǒu) + Object

Location TypeExample Sentence
On a Surface桌子上有一本书。; Zhuōzi shàng yǒu yì běn shū.; There is a book on the table.
Inside a Place学校里有很多学生。; Xuéxiào lǐ yǒu hěn duō xuésheng.; There are many students in the school.
Spatial Direction前面有一家咖啡馆。; Qiánmiàn yǒu yì jiā kāfēiguǎn.; There is a coffee shop up ahead.

Comparison: Existence (有) vs. Location (在)​

VerbPatternInformation FocusExample Sentence
有 (Existence)Location + 有 + ItemWhat is at the place (new item)桌上有手机。; Zhuō shàng yǒu shǒujī.; There is a phone on the table.
在 (Location)Item + 在 + LocationWhere a known item is (new location)手机在桌上。; Shǒujī zài zhuō shàng.; The phone is on the table.

3. Partitives & Subsets: 有的 vs. 有些​

Both 有的 (yǒude) and 有些 (yǒuxiē) express "some", but they differ in sentence structure, nuance, and part of speech:

Feature有的 (yǒude)有些 (yǒuxiē)
Core Meaning"Certain ones / some (out of a defined group)""Some / a few (indefinite quantity)"
Paired PatternStandard for 有的……有的…… (some..., others...)Possible (有些……有些……), but less common
Standalone Subject✔️ Can stand completely alone without a noun⚠️ Prefers pairing with a noun (e.g., 有些人)
Modifying Adjectives❌ Never (Cannot mean "somewhat")✔️ Yes (Synonymous with 有点儿, meaning "a bit / somewhat")

Difference 1: The Correlative Pattern "Some..., Others..."​

When contrasting different members of a group performing distinct actions, 有的……，有的…… is the standard, preferred choice. It can stand alone as a pronoun without repeating the noun:

Formula: (Subject Group) + 有的 + Predicate 1，有的 + Predicate 2

  • 我们班的学生，有的喜欢看书，有的喜欢运动。; Wǒmen bān de xuésheng, yǒude xǐhuan kànshū, yǒude xǐhuan yùndòng.; In our class, some students like reading, and others like sports.
  • 有的人想去，有的人不想去。; Yǒude rén xiǎng qù, yǒude rén bù xiǎng qù.; Some people want to go, while others do not.

Difference 2: Modifying Nouns ("Certain" vs. "A Quantity")​

When modifying nouns, both are grammatically correct, but the nuance shifts:

  • 有的 (yǒude) emphasizes a partitive selection from a specific whole ("certain ones among them"):
    • 有的问题很难。; Yǒude wèntí hěn nán.; Certain questions are very difficult.
  • 有些 (yǒuxiē) contains the plural measure word 些 (xiē), emphasizing an indefinite quantity ("a few / some amount"):
    • 有些人喜欢喝茶。; Yǒuxiē rén xǐhuan hē chá.; Some people like drinking tea.

Difference 3: Adverbial Use (Degree Before Adjectives)​

This is the most critical grammatical difference:

caution

Critical Distinction: Modifying Adjectives

有些 can function as an adverb of degree meaning "somewhat / a little bit", exactly like 有点(儿).
有的 can NEVER modify an adjective directly.

  • ✔️ 我今天有些累。; Wǒ jīntiān yǒuxiē lèi.; I am a bit tired today.
  • ❌ 我今天有的累。 (Completely ungrammatical)

4. Degree Adverb: 有点(儿) (A Bit / Somewhat)​

When paired with an adjective or psychological verb, 有点儿 (yǒudiǎnr) (or southern 有点 / 有一点) acts as an adverb meaning "a little bit" or "kind of".

有点(儿) almost always expresses a negative, undesirable, or complaining nuance (too cold, too tired, too expensive).

Formula: Subject + 有点(儿) + Adjective / Verb

  • 我今天有点儿累。; Wǒ jīntiān yǒudiǎnr lèi.; I am a bit tired today.
  • 这件衣服有点儿贵。; Zhè jiàn yīfu yǒudiǎnr guì.; This piece of clothing is a bit expensive.
  • 这个汉字有点儿难。; Zhè ge hànzì yǒudiǎnr nán.; This Chinese character is a bit difficult.
tip

Pitfall: 有点儿 vs. 一点儿

  • 有点儿 + Adj comes before the adjective and means "a bit too [unfavorable trait]":
    • 今天有点儿冷。; Jīntiān yǒudiǎnr lěng.; Today is a bit chilly.
  • Adj + 一点儿 comes after the adjective and expresses a comparison or request ("a little more..."):
    • 请慢一点儿。; Qǐng màn yìdiǎnr.; Please be a little slower.

1. Comparisons: "As... As..." (有 vs. 没有)​

有 and 没有 evaluate whether something reaches a certain benchmark or standard:

Comparison TypePatternExample Sentence
Negative Comparison
⤷ "A is not as [adj] as B"		A + 没有 + B + (那么) + Adj今天没有昨天冷。; Jīntiān méiyǒu zuótiān lěng.; Today is not as cold as yesterday.

他没有我高。; Tā méiyǒu wǒ gāo.; He is not as tall as me.

中文没有你想象的那么难。; Zhōngwén méiyǒu nǐ xiǎngxiàng de nàme nán.; Chinese is not as hard as you imagine.
Question Benchmark
⤷ "Is A as [adj] as B?"		A + 有 + B + (那么) + Adj + 吗？你有他那么高吗？; Nǐ yǒu tā nàme gāo ma?; Are you as tall as him?

2. High-Frequency Vocabulary Built with 有 vs. 没​

In Chinese, compound adjectives and expressions are formed by pairing nouns with 有 (possessing) or 没 (lacking):

Positive ([有 yǒu])Negative ([没 méi])
有用; yǒuyòng; Useful没用; méiyòng; Useless
有意思; yǒuyìsi; Interesting / Fun没意思; méiyìsi; Boring / Pointless
有钱; yǒuqián; Rich / Wealthy没钱; méiqián; Broke / Has no money
有空; yǒukòng; To have free time没空; méikòng; Busy / No free time
有礼貌; yǒulǐmào; Polite (has manners)没礼貌; méilǐmào; Rude / Impolite
有名; yǒumíng; Famous (has a name)—
有时候; yǒushíhou; Sometimes—
tip

When knocking on a public restroom stall door, stepping into a quiet shop, or checking if a seat is taken:

有人吗？; Yǒu rén ma?; Is anyone here? / Is this occupied?
