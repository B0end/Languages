# Telling Time & Daily Schedule

:::note
Telling time in Mandarin follows a strictly logical **Largest Unit ➔ Smallest Unit** hierarchy:

Part of Day (AM/PM) ➔ [点; diǎn; O'clock](dian3.wav) ➔ [分; fēn; Minute](fen1.wav)

*(e.g., "Afternoon ➔ 3 o'clock ➔ 15 minutes")*
:::

分钟 (fēnzhōng) refers strictly to duration (how long an event lasts).
- Example: 我等了三分钟。(Wǒ děng le sān fēnzhōng.) — "I waited for three minutes."
- Example: 请给我五分钟。(Qǐng gěi wǒ wǔ fēnzhōng.) — "Please give me five minutes."

## 2. The 3 Essential Time Rules & Pitfalls

| Rule | Description | Incorrect ❌ | Correct ✅ | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **1. The "Two" Rule** | Use [两; liǎng; Two (quantity)](liang3.wav), never [二; èr; Two (digit)](er4.wav) for 2 o'clock. | 二点 (*èr diǎn*) | [两点; liǎng diǎn; 2 o'clock](liang3dian3.wav) | Clock hours are a measured quantity. |
| **2. The "Zero" Rule** | For minutes **01–09**, you **must** say [零; líng; 0](ling2.wav). | 四点五分 (*sì diǎn wǔ fēn*) | [四点零五分; sì diǎn líng wǔ fēn; 4:05](si4dian3ling2wu3fen1.wav) | Fills the empty tens place (4:05). |
| **3. Dropping 分 (*fēn*)** | When minutes are **two digits** (10–59), drop [分; fēn; Minute](fen1.wav). | — | [八点二十; bā diǎn èrshí; 8:20](ba1dian3er4shi2.wav) | Dropping *fēn* sounds natural and fluent (8:20). |

## 3. Time Shortcuts: Half and Quarter Hours

Native speakers rely on three common shortcuts rather than counting individual minutes:

| Shortcut | Pattern / Unit | Example |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **:15 (Quarter past)** | [一刻; yí kè; 1 quarter (15 min)](yi2ke4.wav) | [九点一刻; jiǔ diǎn yí kè; 9:15 (quarter past 9)](jiu3dian3yi2ke4.wav) |
| **:30 (Half past)** | [半; bàn; Half (30 min)](ban4.wav) | [四点半; sì diǎn bàn; 4:30 (half past 4)](si4dian3ban4.wav) |
| **:45 (Quarter to)** | [差一刻; chà yí kè; Quarter to](cha4yi2ke4.wav) | [差一刻十点; chà yí kè shí diǎn; 9:45 (quarter to 10)](cha4yi2ke4shi2dian3.wav) |

*(Note: While [三刻; sān kè; 3 quarters (:45)](san1ke4.wav) exists for :45, [差一刻; chà yí kè; Quarter to](cha4yi2ke4.wav) is much more common in daily speech).*

## 4. Periods of the Day (AM vs. PM in Chinese)

Chinese does not use abbreviations like "AM" or "PM". Instead, specify the period of the day before the hour:

> **Order:**  
> **Period of Day** ➔ **[Hour] + [点; diǎn; O'clock](dian3.wav)** ➔ **[Minute] + [分; fēn; Minute](fen1.wav)**

| Time Range | Period of Day | Example |

## 5. Daily Schedule & Word Order (Sentence Patterns)

> ⚠️ **The Golden Word Order Rule:**  
> In English, time usually sits at the end of a sentence (*"I eat lunch at 12:00"*).  
> In Mandarin, **time MUST go BEFORE the verb**:

| Activity | Example Sentence | Literal Word Order |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Wake Up** | [我早上七点起床。; Wǒ zǎoshang qī diǎn qǐchuáng.; I wake up at 7:00 AM.](wo3zao3shang0qi1dian3qi3chuang2.wav) | I + morning 7:00 + wake up |
| **Eat Lunch** | [我们中午十二点吃午饭。; Wǒmen zhōngwǔ shí'èr diǎn chī wǔfàn.; We eat lunch at 12:00 PM.](wo3men0zhong1wu3shi2er4dian3chi1wu3fan4.wav) | We + noon 12:00 + eat lunch |
| **Finish Work** | [他下午六点下班。; Tā xiàwǔ liù diǎn xiàbān.; He finishes work at 6:00 PM.](ta1xia4wu3liu4dian3xia4ban1.wav) | He + afternoon 6:00 + finish work |
| **Go to Sleep** | [我晚上十一点半睡觉。; Wǒ wǎnshang shíyī diǎn bàn shuìjiào.; I go to sleep at 11:30 PM.](wo3wan3shang0shi2yi1dian3ban4shui4jiao4.wav) | I + night 11:30 + go to sleep |
