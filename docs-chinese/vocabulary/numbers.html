Numbers

Counting in Mandarin is completely logical and mathematical. There are no irregular words like "eleven", "twelve", or "twenty"—every number is built from 0 to 10 like building blocks.

You do not need to memorize any new vocabulary—simply add and multiply the core digits.

3. Hundreds (百) & Thousands (千): 100 to 9,999​

Before handling larger numbers, combine place values by stacking units from left to right:

  • 百; bǎi; Hundred (100)
  • 千; qiān; Thousand (1,000)

4. Big Numbers: The 万 (wàn) System (10,000+)​

In English, place values change milestones every three zeroes (thousands ➔ millions ➔ billions).
In Mandarin, place values change milestones every four zeroes (104): the primary milestone is 万 (wàn).

💡 The 4-Zero Mental Shortcut:
Once you pass 9,999, mentally split the number by dropping the last 4 digits to find the number of 万 (wàn):

  • 50,000 ➔ 5 + 0000 ➔ 五万 (wǔwàn)
  • 350,000 ➔ 35 + 0000 ➔ 三十五万 (sānshíwǔwàn)

A. Benchmark Milestone Tiers​

5. The 4 Essential Number Rules​

Rule 1: The "Two" Battle — 二 (èr) vs. 两 (liǎng)​

Mandarin has two different words for the number 2. Using the correct one is crucial:

  • 二; èr; 2 (digits/math): Used for counting digits, mathematics, dates, and codes.
  • 两; liǎng; 2 (quantities): Used for quantities of physical things (placed before a measure word or time unit).
ContextUse 二 (èr) — Digits & SequencesUse 两 (liǎng) — Quantities
Counting vs. Quantity一二三; yī èr sān; 1, 2, 3 (sequence)两个人; liǎng gè rén; Two people
Names/Days vs. Objects星期二; xīngqī'èr; Tuesday两本书; liǎng běn shū; Two books
Codes vs. Clock Time房间 202; fángjiān èr-líng-èr; Room 202两点钟; liǎng diǎn zhōng; 2 o'clock
Tens vs. Thousands+二十二; èrshí'èr; 22两千; liǎngqiān; 2,000

Rule 2: The Zero Bridge (零 - líng)​

Whenever there is an internal empty place value between non-zero digits, you must bridge the gap using 零; líng; 0:

NumberBreakdownWhy 零 (líng) is Needed
一百零五; yībǎi líng wǔ; 105100 + [0] + 5Bridges the empty tens place
一千零八; yīqiān líng bā; 1,0081,000 + [00] + 8A single 零 bridges multiple consecutive empty slots
一万零五十; yīwàn líng wǔshí; 10,05010,000 + [0] + 50Bridges the empty hundreds slot

Rule 3: The Phone & Code Rule, "一" Becomes Yāo (幺)​

When reading out series of individual digits—such as phone numbers, room numbers, ID cards, and bus routes—the digit 一 (yī) is pronounced yāo:

💡 Why?
Over a noisy phone line or radio, yī (1) sounds dangerously similar to qī (7). Pronouncing it as yāo guarantees zero confusion.

ScenarioSpoken NumberNotes
Hotel Room 108一零八; yāo líng bā; 108Hotel / apartment room number
Emergency 110一一零; yāo yāo líng; 110Police emergency hotline / transit route
Phone Number (138-1111)138-1111; yāo sān bā, sì gè yāoPrevents mix-up with 7 (qī)

Rule 4: Ordinal Numbers — The "第" (dì) Prefix​

Turning any cardinal counting number (1, 2, 3) into an ordinal rank (1st, 2nd, 3rd) requires zero irregular forms: simply add the prefix 第; dì; Prefix for ordinals (-th):

Cardinal BaseOrdinal Form (第 + #)
一; yī; 1第一; dì-yī; 1st
二; èr; 2第二; dì-'èr; 2nd
三; sān; 3第三; dì-sān; 3rd
十; shí; 10第十; dì-shí; 10th

6. Fractions, Percentages & Discounts​

In Mandarin, fractions and percentages are built around the concept of parts of a whole: 分之 (fēn zhī), which literally means "parts out of".

A. Fractions ➔ [Denominator] + 分之 + [Numerator]​

Unlike English, which states the top number (numerator) first ("three-quarters"), Mandarin always states the bottom number (denominator) first:

Formula:
Denominator + 分之 (fēn zhī) + Numerator
(Literally: "Out of [Denominator] total parts, take [Numerator]")

FractionLiteral Breakdown
二分之一; èrfēnzhīyī; 1/2 (One half)Out of 2 parts, 1 (or simply 半; bàn; Half)
三分之一; sānfēnzhīyī; 1/3 (One third)Out of 3 parts, 1
四分之三; sìfēnzhīsān; 3/4 (Three-fourths)Out of 4 parts, 3
五分之二; wǔfēnzhī'èr; 2/5 (Two-fifths)Out of 5 parts, 2

B. Percentages ➔ 百分之 (bǎifēnzhī) + [Number]​

Percentages are simply fractions out of 100 ("Out of 100 parts, take X"):

PercentageLiteral Breakdown
百分之一; bǎifēnzhīyī; 1%Out of 100 parts, 1
百分之五十; bǎifēnzhī wǔshí; 50%Out of 100 parts, 50
百分之百; bǎifēnzhī bǎi; 100%Out of 100 parts, 100 (completely)

C. The Chinese Discount Trap: 打折 (dǎzhé)​

⚠️ The Big Cultural Inversion:
In Western stores, a tag saying "20% OFF" tells you what you save.
In China, store discounts tell you what percentage of the original price you pay:

Discount TermMeaning
九折; jiǔ zhé; 10% OFFPay 90% of original price
八折; bā zhé; 20% OFFPay 80% of original price
七五折; qīwǔ zhé; 25% OFFPay 75% of original price
五折; wǔ zhé; 50% OFFPay 50% of original price (Half price)

7. Decimals​

The decimal point is pronounced 点; diǎn; Point / Dot (literally "dot" or "point").

💡 The Reading Rule:

  1. Read the numbers before the decimal point as a standard whole number.
  2. Read the numbers after the decimal point as individual single digits.
DecimalBreakdown
零点五; líng diǎn wǔ; 0.5Zero + dot + five
三点一四; sān diǎn yī sì; 3.14 (Pi)Three + dot + one + four
二十八点六五; èrshíbā diǎn liù wǔ; 28.65Twenty-eight + dot + six + five
百分之零点零一; bǎifēnzhī líng diǎn líng yī; 0.01%Out of 100 + zero + dot + zero + one

8. Cultural Significance & Number Superstitions​

In Chinese culture, numbers carry immense symbolic weight due to homophones—words that sound nearly identical to fortunate or unfortunate words.

A. Lucky & Unlucky Numbers​

NumberSounds Like (Homophone)Cultural Meaning & Real-World Impact
八; bā; 8发; fā; To prosper / get richThe Luckiest Number: License plates, phone numbers, and apartment floors with "8" sell for massive premiums. The Beijing Olympics opened on 08/08/08 at 8:08 PM.
六; liù; 6流; liú; FlowGood Fortune & Success: Associated with things moving smoothly without obstacles (六六大顺; liùliù dàshùn; Everything goes smoothly). Also used as internet slang (666) for "awesome" or "pro".
九; jiǔ; 9久; jiǔ; Long-lasting / EternalEternity & Royalty: Historically reserved for the Emperor (9 dragons, 9,999 rooms in the Forbidden City). Common in romantic contexts (e.g., gifting 99 roses).
四; sì; 4死; sǐ; DeathThe Ultimate Taboo (Tetraphobia): Differing only by tone, "4" is strictly avoided. Many hospitals, hotels, and high-rise residential buildings in China completely skip the 4th, 14th, and 24th floors.

B. Internet Number Slang​

Young Chinese netizens frequently use sequences of digits as phonetic shortcuts in text messaging:

Number CodeSounds LikeUsage Context
520 (wǔ-èr-líng)我爱你; wǒ ài nǐ; I love youMay 20th (5/20) is celebrated as China's modern digital Valentine's Day.
1314 (yī-sān-yī-sì)一生一世; yīshēng yīshì; One life, one lifetimeRepresents eternal love; frequently paired as 5201314 ("I love you for a lifetime").
250 (èr-bǎi-wǔ)(Fool / Idiot)Derived from ancient currency strings; calling someone an èrbǎiwǔ means they are a blockhead.
555 (wǔ-wǔ-wǔ)呜呜呜; wūwūwū; Crying sound (waaah)Used in chat to express sadness, crying, or mock weeping.
