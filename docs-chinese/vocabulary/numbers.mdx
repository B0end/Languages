# Numbers

Counting in Mandarin is completely logical and mathematical. There are no irregular words like *"eleven"*, *"twelve"*, or *"twenty"*—every number is built from **0 to 10** like building blocks.

## 4. Big Numbers: The 万 (*wàn*) System (10,000+)

In English, place values change milestones every **three zeroes** (thousands ➔ millions ➔ billions).  
In Mandarin, place values change milestones every **four zeroes (10<sup>4</sup>)**: the primary milestone is **万 (*wàn*)**.

> 💡 **The 4-Zero Mental Shortcut:**  
> Once you pass 9,999, mentally split the number by **dropping the last 4 digits** to find the number of **万 (*wàn*)**:  
> * `50,000` ➔ `5` + `0000` ➔ **五万** (*wǔwàn*)  
> * `350,000` ➔ `35` + `0000` ➔ **三十五万** (*sānshíwǔwàn*)

## 5. The 4 Essential Number Rules

---

### Rule 1: The "Two" Battle — 二 (*èr*) vs. 两 (*liǎng*)

Mandarin has two different words for the number 2. Using the correct one is crucial:
* [二; èr; 2 (digits/math)](er4.wav): Used for **counting digits, mathematics, dates, and codes**.
* [两; liǎng; 2 (quantities)](liang3.wav): Used for **quantities of physical things** (placed before a measure word or time unit).

| Context | Use 二 (*èr*) — Digits & Sequences | Use 两 (*liǎng*) — Quantities |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Counting vs. Quantity** | [一二三; yī èr sān; 1, 2, 3 (sequence)](yi1er4san1.wav) | [两个人; liǎng gè rén; Two people](liang3ge4ren2.wav) |
| **Names/Days vs. Objects** | [星期二; xīngqī'èr; Tuesday](xing1qi1er4.wav) | [两本书; liǎng běn shū; Two books](liang3ben3shu1.wav) |
| **Codes vs. Clock Time** | [房间 202; fángjiān èr-líng-èr; Room 202](er4ling2er4.wav) | [两点钟; liǎng diǎn zhōng; 2 o'clock](liang3dian3zhong1.wav) |
| **Tens vs. Thousands+** | [二十二; èrshí'èr; 22](er4shi2er4.wav) | [两千; liǎngqiān; 2,000](liang3qian1.wav) |

---

### Rule 2: The Zero Bridge (零 - *líng*)

Whenever there is an **internal empty place value** between non-zero digits, you **must** bridge the gap using [零; líng; 0](ling2.wav):

| Number | Breakdown | Why 零 (*líng*) is Needed |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| [一百零五; yībǎi líng wǔ; 105](yi1bai3ling2wu3.wav) | 100 + [0] + 5 | Bridges the empty tens place |
| [一千零八; yīqiān líng bā; 1,008](yi1qian1ling2ba1.wav) | 1,000 + [00] + 8 | A single 零 bridges multiple consecutive empty slots |
| [一万零五十; yīwàn líng wǔshí; 10,050](yi1wan4ling2wu3shi2.wav) | 10,000 + [0] + 50 | Bridges the empty hundreds slot |

### Rule 3: The Phone & Code Rule, "一" Becomes *Yāo* (幺)

When reading out series of individual digits—such as **phone numbers, room numbers, ID cards, and bus routes**—the digit **一** (*yī*) is pronounced **yāo**:

> 💡 **Why?**  
> Over a noisy phone line or radio, **yī** (1) sounds dangerously similar to **qī** (7). Pronouncing it as **yāo** guarantees zero confusion.

| Scenario | Spoken Number | Notes |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Hotel Room 108** | [一零八; yāo líng bā; 108](yao1ling2ba1.wav) | Hotel / apartment room number |
| **Emergency 110** | [一一零; yāo yāo líng; 110](yao1yao1ling2.wav) | Police emergency hotline / transit route |
| **Phone Number (138-1111)** | [138-1111; yāo sān bā, sì gè yāo](phone_yao.wav) | Prevents mix-up with 7 (*qī*) |

---

### Rule 4: Ordinal Numbers — The "第" (*dì*) Prefix

Turning any cardinal counting number (1, 2, 3) into an ordinal rank (*1st, 2nd, 3rd*) requires zero irregular forms: simply add the prefix [第; dì; Prefix for ordinals (-th)](di4.wav):

## 6. Fractions, Percentages & Discounts

In Mandarin, fractions and percentages are built around the concept of **parts of a whole**: **分之 (*fēn zhī*)**, which literally means *"parts out of"*.

---

### A. Fractions ➔ `[Denominator] + 分之 + [Numerator]`

Unlike English, which states the top number (numerator) first (*"three-quarters"*), Mandarin **always states the bottom number (denominator) first**:

> **Formula:**  
> **Denominator** + **分之 (*fēn zhī*)** + **Numerator**  
> *(Literally: "Out of [Denominator] total parts, take [Numerator]")*

| Fraction | Literal Breakdown |
| :---: | :--- |
| [二分之一; èrfēnzhīyī; 1/2 (One half)](er4fen1zhi1yi1.wav) | Out of 2 parts, 1 *(or simply [半; bàn; Half](ban4.wav))* |
| [三分之一; sānfēnzhīyī; 1/3 (One third)](san1fen1zhi1yi1.wav) | Out of 3 parts, 1 |
| [四分之三; sìfēnzhīsān; 3/4 (Three-fourths)](si4fen1zhi1san1.wav) | Out of 4 parts, 3 |
| [五分之二; wǔfēnzhī'èr; 2/5 (Two-fifths)](wu3fen1zhi1er4.wav) | Out of 5 parts, 2 |

---

### B. Percentages ➔ `百分之 (bǎifēnzhī) + [Number]`

Percentages are simply fractions out of 100 (*"Out of 100 parts, take X"*):

| Percentage | Literal Breakdown |
| :---: | :--- |
| [百分之一; bǎifēnzhīyī; 1%](bai3fen1zhi1yi1.wav) | Out of 100 parts, 1 |
| [百分之五十; bǎifēnzhī wǔshí; 50%](bai3fen1zhi1wu3shi2.wav) | Out of 100 parts, 50 |
| [百分之百; bǎifēnzhī bǎi; 100%](bai3fen1zhi1bai3.wav) | Out of 100 parts, 100 *(completely)* |

---

### C. The Chinese Discount Trap: 打折 (*dǎzhé*)

> ⚠️ **The Big Cultural Inversion:**  
> In Western stores, a tag saying **"20% OFF"** tells you what you **save**.  
> In China, store discounts tell you what percentage of the original price you **pay**:

| Discount Term | Meaning |
| :---: | :--- |
| [九折; jiǔ zhé; 10% OFF](jiu3zhe2.wav) | Pay 90% of original price |

| [七五折; qīwǔ zhé; 25% OFF](qi1wu3zhe2.wav) | Pay 75% of original price |
| [五折; wǔ zhé; 50% OFF](wu3zhe2.wav) | Pay 50% of original price *(Half price)* |

## 8. Cultural Significance & Number Superstitions

In Chinese culture, numbers carry immense symbolic weight due to homophones—words that sound nearly identical to fortunate or unfortunate words.

---

### A. Lucky & Unlucky Numbers

| Number | Sounds Like (Homophone) | Cultural Meaning & Real-World Impact |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| [八; bā; 8](ba1.wav) | [发; fā; To prosper / get rich](fa1.wav) | **The Luckiest Number:** License plates, phone numbers, and apartment floors with "8" sell for massive premiums. The Beijing Olympics opened on **08/08/08 at 8:08 PM**. |
| [六; liù; 6](liu4.wav) | [流; liú; Flow](liu2.wav) | **Good Fortune & Success:** Associated with things moving smoothly without obstacles ([六六大顺; liùliù dàshùn; Everything goes smoothly](liu4liu4da4shun4.wav)). Also used as internet slang (**666**) for *"awesome"* or *"pro"*. |
| [九; jiǔ; 9](jiu3.wav) | [久; jiǔ; Long-lasting / Eternal](jiu3.wav) | **Eternity & Royalty:** Historically reserved for the Emperor (9 dragons, 9,999 rooms in the Forbidden City). Common in romantic contexts (e.g., gifting 99 roses). |
| [四; sì; 4](si4.wav) | [死; sǐ; Death](si3.wav) | **The Ultimate Taboo (Tetraphobia):** Differing only by tone, "4" is strictly avoided. Many hospitals, hotels, and high-rise residential buildings in China **completely skip the 4th, 14th, and 24th floors**. |
