Telling Time & Daily Schedule

note

Telling time in Mandarin follows a strictly logical Largest Unit ➔ Smallest Unit hierarchy:

Part of Day (AM/PM) ➔ 点; diǎn; O'clock ➔ 分; fēn; Minute

(e.g., "Afternoon ➔ 3 o'clock ➔ 15 minutes")

分钟 (fēnzhōng) refers strictly to duration (how long an event lasts).

  • Example: 我等了三分钟。(Wǒ děng le sān fēnzhōng.) — "I waited for three minutes."
  • Example: 请给我五分钟。(Qǐng gěi wǒ wǔ fēnzhōng.) — "Please give me five minutes."

1. The Core Formulas​

ConceptFormulaExample Sentence
Asking the Time—现在几点了？; Xiànzài jǐ diǎn le?; What time is it now?
Standard Time[Hr] + 点 + [Min] + 分三点十分; sān diǎn shí fēn; 3:10

2. The 3 Essential Time Rules & Pitfalls​

RuleDescriptionIncorrect ❌Correct ✅Why?
1. The "Two" RuleUse 两; liǎng; Two (quantity), never 二; èr; Two (digit) for 2 o'clock.二点 (èr diǎn)两点; liǎng diǎn; 2 o'clockClock hours are a measured quantity.
2. The "Zero" RuleFor minutes 01–09, you must say 零; líng; 0.四点五分 (sì diǎn wǔ fēn)四点零五分; sì diǎn líng wǔ fēn; 4:05Fills the empty tens place (4:05).
3. Dropping 分 (fēn)When minutes are two digits (10–59), drop 分; fēn; Minute.—八点二十; bā diǎn èrshí; 8:20Dropping fēn sounds natural and fluent (8:20).

3. Time Shortcuts: Half and Quarter Hours​

Native speakers rely on three common shortcuts rather than counting individual minutes:

ShortcutPattern / UnitExample
:15 (Quarter past)一刻; yí kè; 1 quarter (15 min)九点一刻; jiǔ diǎn yí kè; 9:15 (quarter past 9)
:30 (Half past)半; bàn; Half (30 min)四点半; sì diǎn bàn; 4:30 (half past 4)
:45 (Quarter to)差一刻; chà yí kè; Quarter to差一刻十点; chà yí kè shí diǎn; 9:45 (quarter to 10)

(Note: While 三刻; sān kè; 3 quarters (:45) exists for :45, 差一刻; chà yí kè; Quarter to is much more common in daily speech).

4. Periods of the Day (AM vs. PM in Chinese)​

Chinese does not use abbreviations like "AM" or "PM". Instead, specify the period of the day before the hour:

Order:
Period of Day ➔ [Hour] + 点; diǎn; O'clock ➔ [Minute] + 分; fēn; Minute

Time RangePeriod of DayExample
1:00 AM – 5:59 AM凌晨; língchén; Early morning凌晨三点; língchén sān diǎn; 3:00 AM
6:00 AM – 8:59 AM早上; zǎoshang; Morning早上八点; zǎoshang bā diǎn; 8:00 AM
9:00 AM – 11:59 AM上午; shàngwǔ; Late morning上午十点半; shàngwǔ shí diǎn bàn; 10:30 AM
12:00 PM – 12:59 PM中午; zhōngwǔ; Noon中午十二点; zhōngwǔ shí'èr diǎn; 12:00 PM
1:00 PM – 5:59 PM下午; xiàwǔ; Afternoon下午三点一刻; xiàwǔ sān diǎn yí kè; 3:15 PM
6:00 PM onwards晚上; wǎnshang; Evening / Night晚上九点; wǎnshang jiǔ diǎn; 9:00 PM

5. Daily Schedule & Word Order (Sentence Patterns)​

⚠️ The Golden Word Order Rule:
In English, time usually sits at the end of a sentence ("I eat lunch at 12:00").
In Mandarin, time MUST go BEFORE the verb:

ActivityExample SentenceLiteral Word Order
Wake Up我早上七点起床。; Wǒ zǎoshang qī diǎn qǐchuáng.; I wake up at 7:00 AM.I + morning 7:00 + wake up
Eat Lunch我们中午十二点吃午饭。; Wǒmen zhōngwǔ shí'èr diǎn chī wǔfàn.; We eat lunch at 12:00 PM.We + noon 12:00 + eat lunch
Finish Work他下午六点下班。; Tā xiàwǔ liù diǎn xiàbān.; He finishes work at 6:00 PM.He + afternoon 6:00 + finish work
Go to Sleep我晚上十一点半睡觉。; Wǒ wǎnshang shíyī diǎn bàn shuìjiào.; I go to sleep at 11:30 PM.I + night 11:30 + go to sleep
