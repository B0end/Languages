# Phonetics

In Spanish, vowels are always "pure" (monophthongs), meaning your mouth doesn't move while saying them. French vowels are also **pure**, but there are roughly **16 of them** (similar to the vowel variety in English).

## Oral Vowels Similar to Spanish
* **/a/**: Pure open vowel, like the Spanish "a".
  * [papa;; papá / dad](papa.wav)
* **/i/**: Pure closed vowel, like the Spanish "i" or English "ee" (without gliding).
  * [vie;; vida / life](vie.wav)
  * [ici;; aquí / here](ici.wav)
* **/u/** (Spelled **ou**): Sounds like the Spanish "u" or English "oo" in *food*.
  * [vous;; usted / you](vous.wav)
  * [tout;; todo / all](tout.wav)

## The Front-Rounded Vowels
These sounds do not exist in Spanish or standard English. You produce them by combining a Spanish tongue position with rounded lips:

* **/y/** (Spelled **u**): Position your tongue to say the Spanish **"I"**, keep it completely still, and round your lips tightly as if saying **"U"**.
  * Contrast: [tu;; tú / you](tu.wav) vs. [tout;; todo / all](tout.wav)
  * [musique;; música / music](musique.wav)
* **/ø/** (Closed, spelled **eu** / **œu**): Tongue in Spanish **"E"** position + round your lips like **"O"**.
  * [deux;; dos / two](deux.wav)
  * [peu;; poco / little](peu.wav)
* **/œ/** (Open, spelled **eu** / **œu**): Similar to the vowel sound in English *bird* (non-rhotic), but with rounded lips.
  * [peur;; miedo / fear](peur.wav)
  * [sœur;; hermana / sister](soeur.wav)

## The Nasal Vowels
Air must escape through the nose and the mouth at the same time. **Do not pronounce the letters 'N' or 'M'** at the end.

* **/ɑ̃/** (Spelled *an, en, am, em*): Spanish "A" directed through the nose.
  * [sans;; sin / without](sans.wav)
  * [enfant;; niño / child](enfant.wav)
* **/ɔ̃/** (Spelled *on, om*): Spanish "O" directed through the nose.
  * [bon;; bueno / good](bon.wav)
  * [monde;; mundo / world](monde.wav)
* **/ɛ̃/** (Spelled *in, ain, ein, im*): Spanish "E" (opened towards English *cat*) directed through the nose.
  * [vin;; vino / wine](vin.wav)
  * [pain;; pan / bread](pain.wav)
* **/œ̃/** (Spelled *un, um*): In modern everyday French, this has practically merged with **/ɛ̃/**.
  * [un;; un / one](un.wav)

## Consonants

#### A. Consonants to Borrow from English
* **/ʃ/** (Spelled **ch**): Exactly like the English **"sh"** in *shoe* (avoid the Spanish "ch" sound).
  * [chocolat;; chocolate / chocolate](chocolat.wav)
  * [chien;; perro / dog](chien.wav)
* **/ʒ/** (Spelled **j** or **g + e/i**): The sound of the "s" in English *vi**s**ion* or *mea**s**ure*.
  * [je;; yo / I](je.wav)
  * [manger;; comer / to eat](manger.wav)
* **/z/**: The voiced "s" found in English *bu**zz*** (does not exist in standard Spanish).
  * [zéro;; cero / zero](zéro.wav)
  * [maison;; casa / house](maison.wav)

#### B. Consonants to Borrow from Spanish
* **P, T, K (Unaspirated):** Do **not** add a puff of air like in English (*pʰ*, *tʰ*, *kʰ*). Keep them clean and dry like in Spanish.
  * [Paris;; París / Paris](Paris.wav)
  * [café;; café / coffee](café.wav)
* **T and D (Dental stops):** Place your tongue on the back of your upper front teeth (Spanish style), not on the alveolar ridge behind them (English style).
  * [table;; mesa / table](table.wav)
  * [dent;; diente / tooth](dent.wav)

#### C. The French "R" (/ʁ/)
Do not roll your tongue (no Spanish *carro*) and do not curl it back (no English *red*). 
* **The trick:** It is articulated in the throat at the same location as the soft **Spanish "J"** (as in *jamón*), but your vocal cords must vibrate (voiced uvular fricative), like gargling water gently.
  * [rouge;; rojo / red](rouge.wav)
  * [merci;; gracias / thank you](merci.wav)

## Rhythm, Stress, and Connected Speech

### A. Syllabic Cadence (Think Spanish)
French is **syllable-timed** just like Spanish. Every syllable carries a similar length and weight (unlike English, which shortens unstressed syllables to a *schwa*).

### B. Phrase Stress (L'accent tonique)
Unlike Spanish and English, **individual words do not have their own stressed syllable.** The stress always falls gently on the **final syllable of a rhythm group (clause/idea)**.

* [Le président;; El presidente / The president](Le_president.wav) *(Stress on -DENT)*
* [Le président de la République;; The president of the Republic](Le_president_de_la_Republique.wav) *(Stress moves entirely to -BLIQUE)*

### C. Enchaînement and Liaison (The Flow)
French avoids harsh stops between words to create a continuous stream of sound.

1. **Enchaînement vocalique/consonantique:** A pronounced consonant naturally links to the next vowel sound.
   * [elle aime;; ella ama / she loves](elle_aime.wav) *(Pronounced as: è-lèm)*

2. **Liaison:** A final consonant that is normally **silent** is pronounced when the following word begins with a vowel sound.
   * Normal silent 'S': [les;; los / the](les.wav)
   * Linked 'S' becomes a /z/: [les amis;; los amigos / the friends](les_amis.wav)
   * Normal silent 'T': [petit;; pequeño / small](petit.wav)
   * Linked 'T' carries over: [un petit enfant;; un niño pequeño / a small child](un_petit_enfant.wav)
