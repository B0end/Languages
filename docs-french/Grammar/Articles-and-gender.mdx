# Articles and gender

Because you speak Spanish, **grammatical gender is already natural to you**—unlike native English speakers who struggle with the idea of objects having a gender. In French, just like in Spanish, every single noun is either **masculine** or **feminine**.

However, there are two distinct areas where French behaves differently:
1. **Gender doesn't always match Spanish:** Words that are feminine in Spanish might be masculine in French (and vice versa).
2. **Partitive Articles (*du, de la, des*):** In Spanish and English, you can say *"Bebo agua"* or *"I drink water"* without an article. French **forbids** this—you must use a partitive article (*"Je bois de l'eau"*).

---

### 1. Gender: Predictable Endings & The Spanish False Friends

French does not have clean *-o* (masculine) and *-a* (feminine) endings like Spanish. Instead, you look at word endings (suffixes) to guess the gender.

#### A. Reliable Gender Endings
* **Typically Feminine:**
  * **-tion / -sion:** [la nation;; la nación / the nation](la_nation.wav)
  * **-té:** [la liberté;; la libertad / freedom](la_liberte.wav)
  * **-ence / -ance:** [la différence;; la diferencia / the difference](la_difference.wav)
  * **-ette:** [la bicyclette;; la bicicleta / the bicycle](la_bicyclette.wav)
* **Typically Masculine:**
  * **-age:** [le voyage;; el viaje / the trip](le_voyage.wav)
  * **-ment:** [le moment;; el momento / the moment](le_moment.wav)
  * **-eau:** [le château;; el castillo / the castle](le_chateau.wav)
  * **-isme:** [le tourisme;; el turismo / tourism](le_tourisme.wav)

#### B. The Spanish Gender Trap (False Friends)
Always memorize nouns with their article to avoid carrying Spanish genders over:

* [la mer;; el mar / the sea](la_mer.wav) *(Feminine in French, Masculine in Spanish)*
* [le lait;; la leche / the milk](le_lait.wav) *(Masculine in French, Feminine in Spanish)*
* [la fin;; el fin / the end](la_fin.wav) *(Feminine in French, Masculine in Spanish)*
* [le problème;; el problema / the problem](le_probleme.wav) *(Masculine in both, despite ending in -e)*
* [la voiture;; el coche (o el auto) / the car](la_voiture.wav) *(Feminine in French, Masculine in Spanish)*

### 2. Definite Articles (The / El, La, Los, Las)

These refer to specific things or universal concepts.

| | Masculine | Feminine | Before Vowel / Silent H |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Singular** | **le** | **la** | **l'** (Elision) |
| **Plural** | **les** | **les** | **les** (Causes Liaison /z/) |

* **Masculine:** [le livre;; el libro / the book](le_livre.wav)
* **Feminine:** [la table;; la mesa / the table](la_table.wav)
* **Before a vowel (Elision):** Both *le* and *la* contract into **l'**:
  * [l'homme;; el hombre / the man](l_homme.wav)
  * [l'eau;; el agua / the water](l_eau.wav)
* **Plural:** Unlike Spanish (*los* / *las*), French has only **one** plural form:
  * [les livres;; los libros / the books](les_livres.wav)
  * [les tables;; las mesas / the tables](les_tables.wav)
  * [les enfants;; los niños / the children](les_enfants.wav) *(Liaison makes the 's' sound like a /z/: /le.zɑ̃.fɑ̃/)*

---

### 3. Indefinite Articles (A, An, Some / Un, Una, Unos, Unas)

These refer to non-specific items.

| | Masculine | Feminine |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Singular** | **un** | **une** |
| **Plural** | **des** | **des** |

* **Masculine:** [un café;; un café / a coffee](un_cafe.wav)
* **Feminine:** [une pomme;; una manzana / an apple](une_pomme.wav)
* **Plural:** In English, you can just say *"I see cars"*, and in Spanish *"Veo coches"*. In French, the plural article **des** is **mandatory**:
  * [des amis;; unos amigos (o amigos) / friends (or some friends)](des_amis.wav)
  * [des problèmes;; unos problemas (o problemas) / problems (or some problems)](des_problemes.wav)

---

### 4. Partitive Articles (Unspecified Quantities: "Some")

This is where native Spanish speakers make the most mistakes. In Spanish, uncountable quantities don't need an article: *"Quiero pan"* or *"Tengo paciencia"*. 

In French, you **must quantify uncountables** using partitive articles:

| Form | Gender / Context | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **du** | Masculine singular | [du pain;; pan / bread (some bread)](du_pain.wav) |
| **de la**| Feminine singular | [de la bière;; cerveza / beer (some beer)](de_la_biere.wav) |
| **de l'**| Before vowel / silent H | [de l'argent;; dinero / money (some money)](de_l_argent.wav) |
| **des** | Plural uncountables | [des pâtes;; pasta / pasta (some pasta)](des_pates.wav) |

* [Je bois de l'eau;; Bebo agua / I drink water](Je_bois_de_l_eau.wav)
* [Je mange du fromage;; Como queso / I eat cheese](Je_mange_du_fromage.wav)

> 💡 **The Negative Rule:** In a negative sentence, all partitive and indefinite articles (*du, de la, des, un, une*) turn into simply **de** (or **d'** before a vowel):
> * [Je mange du pain;; Yo como pan / I eat bread](Je_mange_du_pain.wav)
> * [Je ne mange pas de pain;; No como pan / I do not eat bread](Je_ne_mange_pas_de_pain.wav)

### 5. Contracted Articles (Prepositions + Definite Articles)

Just like Spanish combines **a + el = al** and **de + el = del**, French combines prepositions **à** (to/at) and **de** (of/from) with definite articles:

#### A. Contractions with À (to / at)
* **à + le = au:** [au supermarché;; al supermercado / to the supermarket](au_supermarche.wav)
* **à + les = aux:** [aux toilettes;; al baño (a los servicios) / to the bathroom](aux_toilettes.wav)
* *No contraction with feminine or vowels:* [à la plage;; a la playa / to the beach](a_la_plage.wav), [à l'hôtel;; al hotel / to the hotel](a_l_hotel.wav)

#### B. Contractions with DE (of / from)
* **de + le = du:** [du président;; del presidente / of the president](du_president.wav)
* **de + les = des:** [des voisins;; de los vecinos / of the neighbors](des_voisins.wav)
* *No contraction with feminine or vowels:* [de la reine;; de la reina / of the queen](de_la_reine.wav), [de l'école;; de la escuela / of the school](de_l_ecole.wav)
