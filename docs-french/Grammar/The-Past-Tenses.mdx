# The Past Tenses

You have a massive advantage here. English speakers struggle for years to understand the conceptual difference between the **Passé Composé** and the **Imparfait**. 

For a Spanish speaker, this distinction is **already in your brain**:
* **Passé Composé** = *Pretérito Perfecto / Indefinido* (Specific, completed actions: *"comí"* / *"he comido"*).
* **Imparfait** = *Pretérito Imperfecto* (Habitual actions, descriptions, background: *"comía"*).

Here is how past tenses work in French.

---

### 1. Le Passé Composé (Completed Actions)

In spoken and modern written French, the **Passé Composé** has completely replaced the literary simple past (*passé simple*). You use it whenever an action is **finished, dated, or counted**.

#### The Formula:
Subject + Auxiliary (AVOIR or ÊTRE in present) + Past Participle

#### A. Regular Past Participles
* **-ER verbs $\rightarrow$ -é:** [parlé;; hablado / spoken](parle.wav), [mangé;; comido / eaten](mange.wav)
* **-IR verbs $\rightarrow$ -i:** [fini;; terminado / finished](fini.wav), [choisi;; elegido / chosen](choisi.wav)
* **-RE verbs $\rightarrow$ -u:** [vendu;; vendido / sold](vendu.wav), [attendu;; esperado / waited](attendu.wav)

---

#### B. The Choice of Auxiliary: AVOIR vs. ÊTRE
In Spanish, you only ever use *haber* (*"he comido", "he ido"*). In French, you must choose between **Avoir** and **Être**.

##### 1. Most verbs use AVOIR (No agreement with subject):
* [J'ai parlé;; Hablé (o he hablado) / I spoke (or have spoken)](J_ai_parle.wav)
* [Tu as mangé;; Comiste (o has comido) / You ate](Tu_as_mange.wav)
* [Nous avons fini;; Terminamos (o hemos terminado) / We finished](Nous_avons_fini.wav)

##### 2. A specific group uses ÊTRE:
Verbs of **movement/change of state** (often taught via the acronym *DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP*) and **ALL reflexive verbs** (*se lever, se laver*).

> ⚠️ **The Agreement Rule:** When using **Être**, the participle behaves like an adjective and **must agree in gender (-e) and number (-s)** with the subject:

* [Il est allé;; Él fue (o ha ido) / He went](Il_est_alle.wav)
* [Elle est allée;; Ella fue (o ha ido) / She went](Elle_est_allee.wav) *(Adds feminine **-e**; sounds identical)*
* [Ils sont partis;; Ellos se fueron (o han partido) / They left](Ils_sont_partis.wav) *(Adds plural **-s**)*
* [Elles sont arrivées;; Ellas llegaron / They arrived](Elles_sont_arrivees.wav) *(Adds **-es** for feminine plural)*

##### 3. All Reflexive Verbs use Être:
* [Je me suis réveillé;; Me desperté / I woke up](Je_me_suis_reveille.wav)
* [Elle s'est couchée;; Ella se acostó / She went to bed](Elle_s_est_couchee.wav)

---

### 2. L'Imparfait (Descriptions, Habits, Context)

Use the **Imparfait** for ongoing past actions, habitual routines (*"used to"*), descriptions of weather, emotions, and age.

#### How to build it:
Take the **NOUS** form of the present tense, remove **-ons**, and add:
**-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient**

* Example with [parler;; hablar / to speak](parler.wav) (*Nous parlons* $\rightarrow$ root `parl-`):

| Pronoun | Form | Example | Phonetic Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Je** | **-ais** | [Je parlais;; Yo hablaba / I used to speak (was speaking)](Je_parlais.wav) | Sounds like /ɛ/ |
| **Tu** | **-ais** | [Tu parlais;; Tú hablabas / You used to speak](Tu_parlais.wav) | Sounds like /ɛ/ |
| **Il / Elle** | **-ait** | [Elle parlait;; Ella hablaba / She used to speak](Elle_parlait.wav) | Sounds like /ɛ/ |
| **Nous** | **-ions** | [Nous parlions;; Nosotros hablábamos / We used to speak](Nous_parlions.wav) | Sounds like /jɔ̃/ |
| **Vous** | **-iez** | [Vous parliez;; Usted hablaba / You used to speak](Vous_parliez.wav) | Sounds like /je/ |
| **Ils / Elles** | **-aient**| [Ils parlaient;; Ellos hablaban / They used to speak](Ils_parlaient.wav) | Sounds like /ɛ/ |

> 💡 **Phonetic shortcut:** Notice that **je, tu, il, and ils** all end in the exact same sound: **/ɛ/** (like the "e" in Spanish *cama* or English *bed*).

* **The only irregular stem is Être** (`ét-`):
  * [J'étais;; Yo era (o estaba) / I was](J_etais.wav)
  * [Il faisait beau;; Hacía buen tiempo / The weather was nice](Il_faisait_beau.wav)

---

### 3. Passé Composé vs. Imparfait in the Same Sentence

Just like in Spanish, the **Imparfait** sets the background scene, and the **Passé Composé** describes the sudden action that interrupts it:

* [Je dormais quand le téléphone a sonné;; Yo dormía cuando el teléfono sonó / I was sleeping when the phone rang](Je_dormais_quand_le_telephone_a_sonne.wav)
  * *Je dormais* = Imparfait (ongoing background).
  * *A sonné* = Passé Composé (punctual interruption).

* [Quand j'étais petit, je suis tombé de vélo;; Cuando era pequeño, me caí de la bicicleta / When I was little, I fell off the bike](Quand_j_etais_petit_je_suis_tombe_de_velo.wav)
  * *J'étais petit* = Imparfait (state/description).
  * *Je suis tombé* = Passé Composé (single completed event).

---

### 4. Le Passé Récent (The "Easy Win" Tense)

Equivalent to the Spanish **acabar de + infinitivo** or English **to have just done something**:

VENIR (in present) + DE + Infinitive

* [Je viens de manger;; Acabo de comer / I just ate](Je_viens_de_manger.wav)
* [Il vient d'arriver;; Él acaba de llegar / He just arrived](Il_vient_d_arriver.wav)
* [Nous venons de partir;; Acabamos de salir / We just left](Nous_venons_de_partir.wav)

---

### Quick Comparison Summary

| Tense | French Example | Spanish Equivalent | English Equivalent | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Passé Récent** | *Je viens de finir* | *Acabo de terminar* | *I just finished* | Immediate past |
| **Passé Composé** | *J'ai fini* | *Terminé / He terminado* | *I finished / I have finished* | Completed, punctual action |
| **Imparfait** | *Je finissais* | *Terminaba* | *I was finishing / used to finish* | Habit, ongoing state, scene-setting |
