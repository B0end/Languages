Articles and gender

Because you speak Spanish, grammatical gender is already natural to you—unlike native English speakers who struggle with the idea of objects having a gender. In French, just like in Spanish, every single noun is either masculine or feminine.

However, there are two distinct areas where French behaves differently:

  1. Gender doesn't always match Spanish: Words that are feminine in Spanish might be masculine in French (and vice versa).
  2. Partitive Articles (du, de la, des): In Spanish and English, you can say "Bebo agua" or "I drink water" without an article. French forbids this—you must use a partitive article ("Je bois de l'eau").

1. Gender: Predictable Endings & The Spanish False Friends​

French does not have clean -o (masculine) and -a (feminine) endings like Spanish. Instead, you look at word endings (suffixes) to guess the gender.

A. Reliable Gender Endings​

  • Typically Feminine:
    • -tion / -sion: la nation;; la nación / the nation
    • -té: la liberté;; la libertad / freedom
    • -ence / -ance: la différence;; la diferencia / the difference
    • -ette: la bicyclette;; la bicicleta / the bicycle
  • Typically Masculine:
    • -age: le voyage;; el viaje / the trip
    • -ment: le moment;; el momento / the moment
    • -eau: le château;; el castillo / the castle
    • -isme: le tourisme;; el turismo / tourism

B. The Spanish Gender Trap (False Friends)​

Always memorize nouns with their article to avoid carrying Spanish genders over:

  • la mer;; el mar / the sea (Feminine in French, Masculine in Spanish)
  • le lait;; la leche / the milk (Masculine in French, Feminine in Spanish)
  • la fin;; el fin / the end (Feminine in French, Masculine in Spanish)
  • le problème;; el problema / the problem (Masculine in both, despite ending in -e)
  • la voiture;; el coche (o el auto) / the car (Feminine in French, Masculine in Spanish)

2. Definite Articles (The / El, La, Los, Las)​

These refer to specific things or universal concepts.

MasculineFeminineBefore Vowel / Silent H
Singularlelal' (Elision)
Pluralleslesles (Causes Liaison /z/)
  • Masculine: le livre;; el libro / the book
  • Feminine: la table;; la mesa / the table
  • Before a vowel (Elision): Both le and la contract into l':
    • l'homme;; el hombre / the man
    • l'eau;; el agua / the water
  • Plural: Unlike Spanish (los / las), French has only one plural form:
    • les livres;; los libros / the books
    • les tables;; las mesas / the tables
    • les enfants;; los niños / the children (Liaison makes the 's' sound like a /z/: /le.zɑ̃.fɑ̃/)

3. Indefinite Articles (A, An, Some / Un, Una, Unos, Unas)​

These refer to non-specific items.

MasculineFeminine
Singularunune
Pluraldesdes
  • Masculine: un café;; un café / a coffee
  • Feminine: une pomme;; una manzana / an apple
  • Plural: In English, you can just say "I see cars", and in Spanish "Veo coches". In French, the plural article des is mandatory:
    • des amis;; unos amigos (o amigos) / friends (or some friends)
    • des problèmes;; unos problemas (o problemas) / problems (or some problems)

4. Partitive Articles (Unspecified Quantities: "Some")​

This is where native Spanish speakers make the most mistakes. In Spanish, uncountable quantities don't need an article: "Quiero pan" or "Tengo paciencia".

In French, you must quantify uncountables using partitive articles:

FormGender / ContextExample
duMasculine singulardu pain;; pan / bread (some bread)
de laFeminine singularde la bière;; cerveza / beer (some beer)
de l'Before vowel / silent Hde l'argent;; dinero / money (some money)
desPlural uncountablesdes pâtes;; pasta / pasta (some pasta)
  • Je bois de l'eau;; Bebo agua / I drink water
  • Je mange du fromage;; Como queso / I eat cheese

💡 The Negative Rule: In a negative sentence, all partitive and indefinite articles (du, de la, des, un, une) turn into simply de (or d' before a vowel):

  • Je mange du pain;; Yo como pan / I eat bread
  • Je ne mange pas de pain;; No como pan / I do not eat bread

5. Contracted Articles (Prepositions + Definite Articles)​

Just like Spanish combines a + el = al and de + el = del, French combines prepositions à (to/at) and de (of/from) with definite articles:

A. Contractions with À (to / at)​

  • à + le = au: au supermarché;; al supermercado / to the supermarket
  • à + les = aux: aux toilettes;; al baño (a los servicios) / to the bathroom
  • No contraction with feminine or vowels: à la plage;; a la playa / to the beach, à l'hôtel;; al hotel / to the hotel

B. Contractions with DE (of / from)​

  • de + le = du: du président;; del presidente / of the president
  • de + les = des: des voisins;; de los vecinos / of the neighbors
  • No contraction with feminine or vowels: de la reine;; de la reina / of the queen, de l'école;; de la escuela / of the school
