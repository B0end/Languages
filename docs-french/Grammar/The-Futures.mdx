# The Futures

Just like Spanish and English, French expresses the future in two primary ways: the **Futur Proche** (Near Future) and the **Futur Simple** (Simple Future), along with the **Futur Antérieur** (Future Perfect).

Your Spanish knowledge makes this effortless because the **formulas, logic, and even the historical root endings are practically identical.**

---

### 1. Le Futur Proche (The Near Future)

This is by far the **most common future used in spoken French**. It is used for actions happening soon, immediate intentions, or things that are clearly about to happen.

#### The Formula:

ALLER (in present) + Infinitive

This is the exact same structure as Spanish (*Voy a + infinitivo*) and English (*I am going to + verb*), **except French drops the "a"**:

* [Je vais manger;; Voy a comer / I am going to eat](Je_vais_manger.wav)
* [Tu vas faire quoi ?;; ¿Qué vas a hacer? / What are you going to do?](Tu_vas_faire_quoi.wav)
* [Nous allons partir;; Vamos a salir / We are going to leave](Nous_allons_partir.wav)
* [Il va pleuvoir;; Va a llover / It is going to rain](Il_va_pleuvoir.wav)

> ⚠️ **The Negative Trap:** In negative sentences, the **ne... pas** wraps around the conjugated verb (*aller*), NOT the infinitive:
> * [Je ne vais pas dormir;; No voy a dormir / I am not going to sleep](Je_ne_vais_pas_dormir.wav)

---

### 2. Le Futur Simple (The Simple Future)

The **Futur Simple** corresponds to English **"will"** and Spanish forms like **"hablaré"**. It is used for long-term projects, official announcements, predictions, and conditional sentences with *si* (if).

#### The Secret Origin of the Endings (The Romance Hack):
In both Spanish and French, the future tense was historically created by taking the **Infinitive** and slapping the present tense of **AVOIR** (*haber*) onto the end:

* Infinitive base ending in **-r** + endings: **-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont**

| Pronoun | Ending | Regular -ER: Parler | Regular -IR: Finir | Regular -RE: Attendre* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Je** | **-ai** (/e/) | [Je parlerai;; Hablaré / I will speak](Je_parlerai.wav) | [Je finirai;; Terminaré / I will finish](Je_finirai.wav) | [J'attendrai;; Esperaré / I will wait](J_attendrai.wav) |
| **Tu** | **-as** (/a/) | [Tu parleras;; Hablarás / You will speak](Tu_parleras.wav) | [Tu finiras;; Terminarás / You will finish](Tu_finiras.wav) | [Tu attendras;; Esperarás / You will wait](Tu_attendras.wav) |
| **Il / Elle**| **-a** (/a/) | [Il parlera;; Él hablará / He will speak](Il_parlera.wav) | [Il finira;; Él terminará / He will finish](Il_finira.wav) | [Il attendra;; Él esperará / He will wait](Il_attendra.wav) |
| **Nous** | **-ons** (/ɔ̃/)| [Nous parlerons;; Hablaremos / We will speak](Nous_parlerons.wav) | [Nous finirons;; Terminaremos / We will finish](Nous_finirons.wav) | [Nous attendrons;; Esperaremos / We will wait](Nous_attendrons.wav) |
| **Vous** | **-ez** (/e/) | [Vous parlerez;; Usted hablará / You will speak](Vous_parlerez.wav) | [Vous finirez;; Usted terminará / You will finish](Vous_finirez.wav) | [Vous attendrez;; Usted esperará / You will wait](Vous_attendrez.wav) |
| **Ils/Elles**| **-ont** (/ɔ̃/)| [Ils parleront;; Ellos hablarán / They will speak](Ils_parleront.wav) | [Ils finiront;; Ellos terminarán / They will finish](Ils_finiront.wav) | [Ils attendront;; Ellos esperarán / They will wait](Ils_attendront.wav) |

*\*Note for -RE verbs: Always drop the final silent **-e** before adding the endings (attendre $\rightarrow$ attendr-).*

---

#### Irregular Stems in Futur Simple (Mirroring Spanish!)
Just like Spanish changes *tener $\rightarrow$ tendr-* or *hacer $\rightarrow$ har-*, French uses irregular stems, but the **endings never change**:

| Infinitive | Future Stem | Example | Spanish Comparison |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Être** | **ser-** | [Je serai;; Seré (o estaré) / I will be](Je_serai.wav) | *ser-é* (Identical!) |
| **Avoir** | **aur-** | [J'aurai;; Tendré (o habré) / I will have](J_aurai.wav) | *habr-é* |
| **Aller** | **ir-** | [J'irai;; Iré / I will go](J_irai.wav) | *ir-é* (Identical!) |
| **Faire** | **fer-** | [Je ferai;; Haré / I will do](Je_ferai.wav) | *har-é* |
| **Pouvoir**| **pourr-** | [Je pourrai;; Podré / I will be able to](Je_pourrai.wav) | *podr-é* |
| **Vouloir**| **voudr-** | [Je voudrai;; Querré / I will want](Je_voudrai.wav) | *querr-é* |
| **Venir** | **viendr-**| [Je viendrai;; Vendré / I will come](Je_viendrai.wav) | *vendr-é* |
| **Savoir** | **saur-** | [Je saurai;; Sabré / I will know](Je_saurai.wav) | *sabr-é* |
| **Voir** | **verr-** | [Je verrai;; Veré / I will see](Je_verrai.wav) | *ver-é* |

### 3. The "Si" Clause: Present + Future

In both Spanish and English, first conditional sentences combine the present tense with the future tense. French does the exact same thing:

* [Si tu étudies, tu réussiras;; Si estudias, aprobarás / If you study, you will succeed](Si_tu_etudies_tu_reussiras.wav)
* [S'il fait beau demain, nous irons à la plage;; Si hace buen tiempo mañana, iremos a la playa / If the weather is nice tomorrow, we will go to the beach](S_il_fait_beau_demain_nous_irons_a_la_plage.wav)

---

### 4. Le Futur Antérieur (The Future Perfect)

Used to express an action that **will have been completed** before another future event takes place.

#### The Formula:

AVOIR or ÊTRE (in Futur Simple) + Past Participle

This completely mirrors Spanish (*Futuro Compuesto: Habré terminado*) and English (*I will have finished*):

* [J'aurai fini à midi;; Habré terminado al mediodía / I will have finished at noon](J_aurai_fini_a_midi.wav)
* [Quand tu arriveras, elle sera déjà partie;; Cuando llegues, ella ya se habrá ido / When you arrive, she will already have left](Quand_tu_arriveras_elle_sera_deja_partie.wav)
